The **Honda City Hatchback RS** is the car that ended the Jazz. When Honda Cars Philippines announced the [Jazz's discontinuation in April 2021](/blog/honda-jazz-pms-guide), the reason wasn't reliability or recalls — it was this car arriving, and Honda not wanting two of its own hatchbacks fighting over the same buyer.

Five years on, it is still here, still sold in a **single RS variant**, and still carrying the most persistent misinformation problem of any Honda in the local lineup: **a large share of what you will read about the "Honda City Hatchback" online describes an engine that was never sold here.** Ours is a 1.5-litre naturally aspirated four. The one in Thailand and Malaysia is a 1.0-litre three-cylinder turbo — or a hybrid. Same badge, same body, different car underneath.

Here is the real ownership picture. This is the City Hatchback companion to our [complete guide to PMS meaning, schedule and cost](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide).

## Key Takeaways

- **PMS is every 10,000 km or 6 months** — Honda Cars Philippines' brand-wide cadence, now verified across six of our Honda guides. Against the 5,000 km rivals, that is **two casa visits a year instead of four.**
- **It is not a turbo.** The PH car is the **1.5L NA DOHC i-VTEC**, 121 PS / 145 Nm. The **1.0 three-cylinder VTEC Turbo** and the **e:HEV hybrid** are Thai and Malaysian variants that never came here — and at least one major PH listing site currently describes our car as "turbocharged" anyway.
- **The 5-star ASEAN NCAP score everyone quotes is the sedan's**, tested in 2020 in **Thai 1.0-litre spec**. There is no separate hatchback rating. Our car does get **6 airbags standard**, which is more than the base spec in that test.
- **Honda SENSING only arrived with the 2024 facelift.** A 2021–2023 used unit does not have it. This is the single most important model-year check on this car.
- **CVT fluid must be Honda HCF-2.** Honda excludes damage from non-equivalent fluid from warranty.
- **Timing chain** — no scheduled belt replacement, ever.
- **134 mm of ground clearance**, against the Jazz's 150 mm. One of the lowest cars on sale here. Plan your humps and ramps accordingly.
- **Honda's PM Cost Calculator is still offline** — we re-checked it on **6 September 2026** and it still reads "temporarily unavailable due to maintenance." That is now the sixth consecutive Honda guide we have written without an official published PMS price.

## Which Model Years Does This Guide Cover? (2021–2026)

This guide covers the **Philippine City Hatchback from its April 2021 launch to the current 2026 car** — the hatchback body of the seventh-generation City, built on the same Honda Global Small Car platform as the [City sedan](/blog/honda-city-pms-guide) and the fourth-generation Jazz.

It has only ever been sold here in **one variant: the 1.5 RS CVT.** There is no S, no V, and no hybrid. What has changed is the equipment:

| Period | What you get | Indicative price |
|---|---|---|
| **April 2021 launch** | 1.5 NA i-VTEC, CVT with paddles, 6 airbags, ULTR seats, **no Honda SENSING** | **₱1,115,000** at launch |
| **2022–2023** | Same car, price drift | around **₱1,153,000** |
| **2024 facelift** | **Honda SENSING added**, wireless CarPlay/Android Auto, walk-away auto lock, milder front bumper trim | **₱1,189,000–₱1,194,000** |
| **2026** | Unchanged mechanically; **30th Anniversary Pearl Edition** added | **₱1,199,000**, anniversary edition **₱1,279,000** |

Two honest notes on that table. **The sources disagree on the current price** — Zigwheels still shows ₱1.189M while AutoDeal lists ₱1,199,000 for the base colour and ₱1,219,000 for Platinum White Pearl. Treat these as indicative and confirm at the dealer.

**Length is also source-conflicted.** Top Gear PH published **4,349 mm** at both the 2021 launch and the 2024 facelift; Zigwheels currently lists **4,369 mm**. A 20 mm difference is exactly what a bumper change produces, so one of those figures is probably stale — but we cannot tell you which, so we are printing both.

One thing worth knowing even though it does not affect your car: **Honda revealed another City and City Hatchback facelift for Thailand in May 2026.** It is not here, and Honda Cars Philippines has not announced it. If you are buying now, you are buying the 2024-facelift car.

## The Spec Sheet Trap: Our Hatchback Is Not Their Hatchback

This deserves its own section, because it is the easiest way to make a bad decision about this car.

| | **Philippines** | **Thailand / Malaysia** |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | **1.5L four-cylinder, naturally aspirated** DOHC i-VTEC | **1.0L three-cylinder VTEC Turbo** |
| Output | **121 PS @ 6,600 rpm, 145 Nm @ 4,300 rpm** | 122 PS @ 5,500 rpm, **173 Nm** |
| Hybrid option | **None** | **e:HEV**, in several trims |
| Variants | **RS only** | Turbo S plus e:HEV V / SV / RS |

The outputs look close on the headline power figure, which is exactly why the confusion survives. They are not close in character: the Thai car makes **173 Nm** low down off a turbo; ours makes **145 Nm at 4,300 rpm** and wants revs.

<Pullquote>When a review praises the low-end torque of a "City Hatchback," check which country wrote it. Ours does not have that engine.</Pullquote>

The practical consequences:

- **Fuel-economy figures from Thai and Malaysian reviews do not transfer.** Different engine, different displacement, different cylinder count.
- **Reliability reports about the 1.0 turbo three-cylinder are irrelevant to you** — the turbo, its injectors and the three-pot's own quirks are simply not parts of your car.
- **Any e:HEV hybrid-battery discussion is not your car either.**

This is the same trap we documented on the [HR-V, where the American car is a different vehicle entirely](/blog/honda-hr-v-pms-guide) from ours. **Check market provenance before trusting any spec sheet or reliability score.** In this case, even a major Philippine listing site currently describes our naturally aspirated car as "turbocharged" — so the trap is not only in foreign sources.

## The Service Schedule: Every 10,000 km or 6 Months

Honda Cars Philippines runs a **10,000 km / 6-month** PMS cadence across the range, and we have now independently confirmed that same interval in our City, Brio, Jazz, BR-V and HR-V guides. It remains Honda's most underrated ownership advantage here.

| Annual distance | City Hatchback visits/yr | A 5,000 km rival's visits/yr |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 km | 1 | 2 |
| 20,000 km | 2 | 4 |
| To 100,000 km | **10 oil changes** | **20 oil changes** |

Against the [Vios](/blog/toyota-vios-pms-guide) and the [Mirage](/blog/mitsubishi-mirage-pms-guide), both on 5,000 km cycles, that gap compounds quietly for as long as you own the car — in money, and in Saturdays.

### Light vs. Heavy (Major) PMS

**Light PMS — roughly ₱5,000–7,000 at the casa.** Engine oil and filter, air cleaner inspection, brakes, tyres and suspension check, fluid top-ups, battery and charging check, tyre rotation, road test.

**Heavy PMS — roughly ₱10,000–18,000**, at the bigger 40,000 km and 80,000 km stops. Everything above, plus the air cleaner element replaced, cabin filter replaced, brake fluid changed, spark plugs at the longer interval, and **the CVT fluid service.**

*These are estimates based on the Honda range and current casa averages, not a published price list.* We want to be precise about why: **Honda's own PM Cost Calculator has been offline since at least June 2026.** We checked it again while writing this, on **6 September 2026**, and it still returns the same message — "temporarily unavailable due to maintenance." No Philippine manufacturer publishes per-model PMS pricing, and Honda's single attempt at it has now been down for roughly three months.

For the same reason, **we are not printing an oil capacity figure.** The L15 family's published capacities conflict across sources, Honda PH publishes nothing model-specific, and a wrong number here costs a reader an engine. Your owner's manual is the authority. This is the sixth Honda guide in a row where we have had to leave that blank, and we would rather leave it blank than guess.

### The CVT Rule

The City Hatchback uses a conventional **belt-and-pulley CVT** — a real CVT, unlike the HR-V RS e:HEV's hybrid transaxle that merely shares the "E-CVT" name.

That means the fluid rule applies in full: **Honda HCF-2, and nothing else.** Honda's own owner's-manual language for this gearbox family states that damage caused by non-equivalent fluid is not covered by warranty. Generic ATF is not a substitute, a cheaper "compatible" fluid is not a substitute, and a CVT that has been run on the wrong fluid usually announces itself as shudder or hesitation long after the receipt is gone.

If you are weighing casa against your suki, our [casa vs. talyer cost and warranty breakdown](/blog/casa-vs-talyer-philippines) covers where the line sits. For this specific job, the line is simple: whoever does it, it gets HCF-2.

## What It Is Actually Like to Own

**The seats are the reason to buy it.** The **ULTR seats** — the Jazz's magic seats under a new name — fold flat for **841 litres**, or flip up to carry tall items upright behind the front seats. **289 litres** with the seats up. This is still the most genuinely useful packaging trick in the segment, and it is the clearest thing the City Hatchback inherited from the car it replaced.

**Real-world fuel economy**, from Top Gear PH's road test of the 2024 car: **9–10 km/L in the city, 16–18 km/L on the highway**, against an advertised best of **21.3 km/L**. Other outlets have reported higher city figures; treat 9–10 in real Metro Manila traffic as the honest planning number.

**Owner sentiment is good, but the sample is small.** Zigwheels PH shows **4.41/5 across 29 reviews** — genuinely positive, and genuinely too few reviews to call it a reliability verdict. We are reporting it as sentiment, not data.

## Known Issues on the City Hatchback

We hold this section to verified, PH-relevant reports only. **There is no systemic mechanical defect pattern on this car**, and we are not going to invent one to fill the space. What is consistently reported:

- **Road and engine noise.** The most repeated criticism across professional reviews — the cabin lets in more road roar and engine noise at highway speed than the Jazz did. Reviewers specifically wished for more insulation. This is a refinement complaint, not a fault.
- **Interior plastics feel a step down** from the previous Jazz and City generations. Again: build-quality perception, not a defect.
- **The infotainment screen sits too flat** and catches sun glare — a real daily annoyance in PH conditions.
- **Adaptive cruise control can be jerky when decelerating**, on the post-2024 SENSING cars.
- **The rear middle seat has a high bump**, which makes it a practical four-seater more than a five-seater on long trips.
- **Ground clearance of 134 mm** is the one that actually costs money. It is lower than the Jazz's 150 mm and among the lowest of anything currently on sale here. Speed humps, deep gutters and steep mall ramps all deserve more caution than you would give a Vios. Nothing about **pang-ilalim** damage is covered by warranty.

### Recall Status

**No Honda Cars Philippines recall names the City Hatchback.** We checked HCPI's published recall listing directly. The City *is* named in older campaigns — the **June 11, 2020 fuel-pump recall** covering 2018–2019 units, and a 2019 airbag campaign — but those cover **earlier City generations, before this hatchback existed.** The hatchback launched in April 2021.

We are phrasing that as *not publicly reported* rather than *does not exist*, because there is no searchable public recall database in the Philippines and HCPI's index page does not always name every affected model — a gap we hit on the [BR-V](/blog/honda-br-v-pms-guide), where only the campaign's detail page named the model, and again on the HR-V. **If you own one, run your VIN through Honda's recall check anyway.** It is free.

## Should You Buy One?

**Buy new** if you want the Jazz's packaging with Honda SENSING and a 10,000 km service interval, and you can live with a low nose and a noisy cabin at speed.

**Buying used** is where the money is, and the rule is short: **2024 or newer for Honda SENSING, or accept a 2021–2023 car without it and pay less.** Those cars look nearly identical. The safety kit is not.

Cross-shopping? The [City sedan](/blog/honda-city-pms-guide) is the same engine, same interval and same HCF-2 rule, with a boot instead of a tailgate and more ground clearance. If you need seven seats on that same drivetrain, the [BR-V costed in full](/blog/honda-br-v-pms-guide) is the one to read. And if you specifically wanted the Jazz, our [Jazz used-buying and maintenance guide](/blog/honda-jazz-pms-guide) explains what it costs to run the car this one replaced.
