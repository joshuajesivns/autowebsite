The **Jaecoo EJ6** is the quiet veteran of the local Jaecoo range. Its first Philippine batch landed in **December 2024** — before the brand had even formally launched here — which makes it the longest-serving of the three models we cover, and the only one that drops petrol entirely.

It also carries the single most concrete ownership perk in the lineup: **two years of free PMS**, bundled with the car. For a brand that publishes no service pricing at all, that turns out to matter more than it first appears.

## Key Takeaways

- **₱1,649,000 (RWD) / ₱1,799,000 (AWD).** First PH units arrived December 2024.
- **RWD: 65.69 kWh LFP, 181 hp, 410 km.** **AWD: 69.77 kWh, ~275 hp, 421 km NEDC.**
- **The AWD costs ₱150,000 more for about 11 km of extra range.** You are buying acceleration, not distance.
- **DC fast charge 30–80% in 30 minutes**; 6.6 kW AC wall box takes about **12.5 hours** for a full charge.
- **Warranty: 7 years / 200,000 km vehicle, 8 years / 200,000 km battery** — the 200,000 km ceiling beats most rivals' 150,000 km.
- **Two years of free PMS**, plus a portable emergency charger and a wall charger, included in the launch package.
- **LFP chemistry** — the right call for Philippine heat and repeated fast charging.
- **225 mm of ground clearance**, which is unusually high for an electric crossover and genuinely useful here.

## Which Model Year Does This Guide Cover? (2024–2026)

This guide covers the EJ6 from the arrival of its first Philippine units in **December 2024** through the **2026** model year, in **RWD** and **AWD** forms. The model is built on Chery's **Tansuo 06** platform — worth knowing, because Chery is Jaecoo's parent and the shared engineering is part of why a brand this new has a car this settled.

EV pricing and trims move faster than anything else on sale, which is exactly why this section exists. Kung 2027 ka na nagbabasa nito, i-check mo muna ang kasalukuyang price list bago ka umasa sa mga numero rito.

## RWD or AWD? The ₱150,000 Question

This is the only real decision the EJ6 asks of you, and the spec sheet answers it more clearly than most.

**What ₱150,000 buys:** a second motor at the front, 94 more horsepower, 165 more Nm, and a battery 4.08 kWh larger.

**What it does not buy:** meaningful extra range. **410 km versus 421 km is roughly 11 km** — about 2.7%. The bigger pack is mostly feeding the bigger motor, not extending your reach.

So the decision is honest and simple: **if you want range, the RWD is the smarter buy.** It is the better value by a wide margin and 410 km covers Philippine use comfortably. Pay for the AWD only if you specifically want the acceleration or the traction, and know that you are paying for those, not for distance.

Worth noting the RWD layout itself. A rear-drive single-motor EV at this price is unusual — most rivals are front-drive — and it gives the EJ6 better weight balance and a tighter turning behaviour than the segment norm.

## Range: What 410 km Actually Means Here

**Both figures are NEDC**, the most optimistic of the common test cycles. Our standing advice on this site is to discount NEDC heavily — the BYD Dolphin's 405 km NEDC claim returned roughly 300 km in TopGear Philippines' real-world testing, a gap of about 26%.

Apply that same discount here and the RWD realistically delivers somewhere near **300–320 km** in mixed Philippine driving with the aircon running. That is not a criticism of the EJ6 specifically — it is how NEDC works for every car quoted under it.

**What 300-ish km buys you in practice:** a full week of Metro Manila or Calabarzon commuting on a single charge for most drivers, or a one-way Manila–Baguio run with charge to spare. Ang kailangan mong isipin ay hindi ang pinakamalayong biyahe mo kada taon, kundi ang karaniwang linggo mo — doon mo makikita kung sapat ito.

## Charging: The Numbers and the Reality

| | |
|---|---|
| DC fast (30–80%) | **30 minutes** |
| AC 6.6 kW wall box (full) | **~12.5 hours** |
| Included | portable emergency charger + wall charger |

**30 minutes for 30–80% on DC is competitive**, and good enough that a provincial trip becomes a coffee stop rather than an ordeal.

**12.5 hours on AC sounds alarming and mostly isn't.** That is a full charge from near-empty, which is not how anyone actually uses an EV daily. Plugging in overnight with 40% remaining takes far less. The pattern that works is a nightly top-up, not a weekly fill.

**The honest PH part:** both numbers are irrelevant if you have nowhere to plug in. Public DC infrastructure is still concentrated in Metro Manila and along a few expressway corridors, and it is improving but not yet something to depend on. The EJ6 bundling a wall charger with the car removes the most common hidden cost, but it cannot give you a parking slot with an outlet. Kung wala kang masasaksakan sa gabi, hindi pa ito ang sasakyan para sa iyo — tingnan mo muna ang [J5 SHS-H na hindi kailangang saksakan](/blog/jaecoo-j5-shs-h-guide).

One genuine advantage worth flagging: **the LFP chemistry suits this market.** Lithium iron phosphate tolerates repeated fast charging and high ambient heat better than NMC, and it is more thermally stable. The trade-offs — slightly more weight, weaker cold-weather performance — are things the Philippines simply does not have to care about.

## Maintenance: Two Free Years, Then a Blank

Here the EJ6 separates itself from its siblings in a way that genuinely matters.

**The launch package includes two years of free PMS.** Across the Jaecoo range — where no Philippine service pricing is published at all — that is the only concrete maintenance commitment a buyer can actually hold. Confirm it is still attached to the unit you are buying, because launch bundles do get quietly withdrawn.

**After those two years, Jaecoo has published nothing.** As of October 2026 we could not source a Philippine PMS schedule or price list for any Jaecoo model, this one included.

The mitigating factor is that **an EV's service list is short by nature**. There is no engine oil, no oil filter, no spark plugs, no timing component, no exhaust, no fuel system. What remains is roughly:

- Multi-point inspection
- Brake fluid (EVs are gentler on pads thanks to regenerative braking, but fluid still degrades on a calendar schedule)
- Battery and motor cooling-loop coolant
- Cabin air filter
- Tyres — and note the **245/55 R19s**, which are a wider and pricier fitment than a typical crossover; budget accordingly
- Software updates

**The honest framing:** the blank in Jaecoo's price list hurts least on this model, because the list of billable items is short and the first two years are covered. Pero sa ikatlong taon, numero pa rin ang kailangan mo — so ask for per-visit pricing in writing, the same as you would on any car. Our [breakdown of how Philippine PMS pricing is structured](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide) is useful for framing that conversation even though it was written around petrol cars.

## TCO vs. a Petrol Equivalent

At ₱1,649,000 the EJ6 RWD sits against well-equipped petrol compact SUVs. The structural comparison:

| | EJ6 RWD | Petrol compact SUV (~₱1.6M) |
|---|---|---|
| Energy cost | residential electricity | pump prices |
| Oil changes | **none** | every 5,000–10,000 km |
| Spark plugs / filters | **none / cabin only** | full schedule |
| Brake wear | reduced by regen | normal |
| First 2 years servicing | **free** (launch package) | owner-paid |
| Warranty | **7 yr / 200,000 km** | commonly 3–5 yr / 100,000 km |
| Battery warranty | 8 yr / 200,000 km | n/a |
| Refuel/recharge time | 30 min DC / overnight AC | 5 minutes |
| Depends on charging access | **yes** | no |

The running-cost case is strong and the warranty case is stronger. **The entire argument rests on one prerequisite** — reliable charging access — and no amount of favourable arithmetic survives its absence. That is the single question to settle before anything else on this page matters.

## Warranty: Two Numbers, Both Unusually Long

- **Vehicle: 7 years / 200,000 km**
- **Battery: 8 years / 200,000 km**

Do not blur these together; they are separate commitments covering separate things.

What stands out is not the years but the **kilometres**. Most EV warranties in this market cap at 150,000 km, and for anyone driving 25,000–30,000 km a year — a Grab driver, a provincial commuter, a long-haul daily — that 50,000 km difference arrives years before the calendar limit does. Sa mataas na mileage, ang 200,000 km ang totoong numero, hindi ang 7 o 8 taon.

## Known Issues: Nothing Verified Locally

The EJ6 has the longest Philippine presence of the three Jaecoo models here, with units on the road since December 2024 — but the local fleet is still small, and **we found no verified owner-defect pattern to report.** We are not going to assemble a plausible-looking list in its place.

Published review commentary notes **noticeable body roll in corners**, and that **most vehicle controls are consolidated into the infotainment screen** — a design choice some drivers dislike on principle. Neither is a defect; both are things to check on a test drive rather than discover later.

The open risks remain structural: service network density outside Metro Manila, parts lead times for a young brand, and no established resale data for an EV from a marque this new.

## Verdict: The Most Complete Jaecoo, If You Can Charge

The EJ6 is the strongest-executed car in the local Jaecoo range. The RWD layout is unusual and good at this money, the LFP pack is the right chemistry for our climate, 225 mm of ground clearance is genuinely practical on Philippine roads, and the 200,000 km warranty ceiling is the real differentiator against rivals that stop at 150,000.

The two free PMS years also quietly solve, for 24 months, the problem that follows every other Jaecoo: nobody outside the dealer network can tell you what servicing costs.

**Buy the RWD if** you can charge where you park and want the best value — 410 km is enough, and the ₱150,000 saved over the AWD buys you almost no extra range. **Buy the AWD only if** you want the performance and traction for their own sake. **Buy neither if** charging is uncertain, in which case the [J5 SHS-H](/blog/jaecoo-j5-shs-h-guide) at ₱999,000 or the plug-in [J7 SHS](/blog/jaecoo-j7-shs-pms-guide) will serve you far better.
