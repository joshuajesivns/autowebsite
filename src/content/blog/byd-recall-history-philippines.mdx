When a brand grows as fast as **BYD** has in the Philippines, the question stops being "magkano?" and starts being "matibay ba?" And the fastest way to answer that honestly is to look at the recall record — not the marketing.

Here's the straight answer up front: **BYD has filed four major recalls since 2024, all of them in China, covering about 484,689 vehicles.** Wala pang isa sa mga iyon ang umabot sa mga unit na ibinenta dito. That's not a defense of the brand — it's just what the records say as of **October 2026**, and below is every figure with the defect spelled out, so you can judge it yourself.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four recalls since 2024, all China-market**, filed with SAMR (the Chinese regulator): **96,714** units (Sept 30, 2024), **115,783** (Oct 17, 2025), **88,981** (Dec 1, 2025), and **183,211** (Sept 21, 2026).
- **The September 2026 brake-pedal recall is BYD's largest ever** — 183,211 Qin and Tang units built between 2014 and 2022.
- **No PH-sold BYD has been recalled.** ACMobility explicitly confirmed this for the 2024 Atto 3/Dolphin recall; the other three cover models or production years never sold here.
- **The Atto 3 is the Yuan Plus in China** — so a China recall naming the Yuan Plus *does* concern your model, just not your unit.
- **A recall is not automatically a red flag.** All four remedies were free, and two were caught before any injury was reported. The worry is a brand with complaints and *no* recall.
- **Check by VIN, not by model year.** Only your authorized dealer's system can tell you definitively.
- Separately, there are **owner-reported faults that are not recalls** — covered at the bottom, clearly labeled, because the difference matters.

## What This Guide Covers (recall records as of October 2026)

Every recall below was filed with China's **State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR)**, the body BYD reports to for its home market. Unit counts and production windows are as published in those filings and reported by the motoring press at the time.

Two limits worth stating plainly:

- **This is a China-market record.** BYD does not publish a single global recall register, so a market without a public recall system can look "clean" simply because nothing gets published. Ang absence ng recall ay hindi katumbas ng absence ng problema.
- **The Philippine picture rests on distributor statements**, not on a local regulator's database. The Philippines has no public vehicle-recall registry comparable to SAMR's or the NHTSA's, so "PH units are not affected" means the distributor said so.

## The Short Answer for Philippine Owners

BYD Cars Philippines relaunched under **AC Motors / ACMobility in 2023**. Every BYD sold new here dates from that point forward — and that single fact does most of the work in explaining why the recall list keeps missing the Philippines.

| Recall | Does it touch PH units? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 30, 2024 — Dolphin, Yuan Plus | **No** | ACMobility confirmed PH units unaffected; BYD stated overseas Atto 3 units are outside the recall |
| Oct 17, 2025 — Tang, Yuan Pro | **No** | Covers 2015–2017 Tang (an older generation than the PH car) and the Yuan Pro, never sold here |
| Dec 1, 2025 — Qin PLUS DM-i | **No** | The Qin has never been offered in the Philippines |
| Sept 21, 2026 — Qin, Tang | **No** | Covers 2014–2022 production, which predates ACMobility's 2023 relaunch |

## Recall 1 — September 30, 2024: Steering Controller, Fire Risk (96,714 units)

This is the one that actually reached Philippine headlines, because it named a car we sell a lot of here.

**Affected:** 96,714 units in total — 87,762 **Dolphin** and **Yuan Plus** units built **February 4 to December 26, 2023**, plus 8,952 **Yuan Plus** units built **November 2, 2022 to June 19, 2023**.

**The defect:** a manufacturing fault in the **column-assist electric power steering (CEPS) controller**. A tooling cover could press against capacitors on the controller's circuit board, creating micro-cracks. Those cracks could widen during normal use, leading to a short circuit, overheating, and — in the worst case — fire.

**The remedy:** BYD dealers fitted insulation pads to the controller at no cost, with owners notified by registered mail, WeChat, and phone.

**Why it matters here:** the **Yuan Plus is the Atto 3**, BYD's volume seller in the Philippines, and the production window overlaps the period when PH imports were already arriving. ACMobility addressed it directly and confirmed that **units sold in the Philippines are not affected**, and BYD separately stated the recall did not cover overseas Atto 3 units. If you own one, the practical note is in our [Atto 3 ownership-cost breakdown](/blog/byd-atto-3-pms-guide) — and the [Dolphin running-cost guide](/blog/byd-dolphin-pms-guide) carries the same recall note for that model.

## Recall 2 — October 17, 2025: Motor Controller and Battery Sealing (115,783 units)

At the time, this was BYD's largest recall. It bundled two unrelated defects on two different models.

**Tang PHEV — 44,535 units**, built **March 28, 2015 to July 28, 2017**. A component design issue could cause the drive-motor controller to malfunction and, in extreme cases, burn out the circuit board.

**Yuan Pro EV — 71,248 units**, built **February 6, 2021 to August 5, 2022**. Battery packs were not sealed consistently in manufacturing, which could let water intrude. Technicians applied a reinforcing sealant to the battery housing, free of charge.

**Why PH units are outside it:** the **Tang** sold here is a far newer generation than the 2015–2017 cars in the filing — the second-generation Tang only debuted globally in 2018, and the Tang on BYD Philippines' current price list is newer still. The **Yuan Pro** was never part of the ACMobility lineup at all.

## Recall 3 — December 1, 2025: Battery Packs on the Qin PLUS DM-i (88,981 units)

**Affected:** 88,981 **Qin PLUS DM-i** plug-in hybrids built **January 2021 to September 2023**.

**The defect:** manufacturing-consistency problems in the power-battery packs that could reduce the battery's output and, in some situations, shut down electric-drive mode entirely — a safety hazard if it happens in traffic.

**The remedy:** an over-the-air update to the diagnostic system. Once installed, a detected anomaly raises a dashboard warning telling the driver to visit a service centre, where faulty packs are replaced free.

**Why it doesn't reach PH:** the **Qin has never been sold in the Philippines**. It's a China-market sedan that sits below the Seal in BYD's domestic range, and ACMobility has never listed it.

This one is still worth reading if you drive a plug-in hybrid here, like the [Seal 5 DM-i](/blog/byd-seal-5-pms-guide) or the [Sealion 6 DM-i](/blog/byd-sealion-6-pms-guide) — not because they're implicated, but because it shows the failure mode BYD now screens for over the air. Ang OTA diagnostics ay hindi lang para sa bagong features; ginagamit na rin iyan para humabol sa defect bago pa ito lumala.

## Recall 4 — September 21, 2026: Brake Pedal Buffer (183,211 units)

BYD's biggest recall to date, and the most recent.

**Affected:** 183,211 **Qin** and **Tang** series vehicles built between **2014 and 2022**.

**The defect:** a bad batch of **brake-pedal stop buffers** — the small component the pedal rests against — that could tear or detach over time. When it fails, the brake lights can stay illuminated even when the driver isn't braking.

**Why that's a real safety issue:** it isn't about stopping power. It's that the cars behind you lose the ability to tell when you're actually braking. A brake light that is always on is, functionally, a brake light that says nothing — exactly the kind of fault that causes a rear-end collision na walang kasalanan ang nasa likod.

**The remedy:** free replacement of the affected component at authorized dealers.

**Why PH units sit outside it:** the production window closes in **2022**, before ACMobility's 2023 relaunch. The Qin was never sold here, and the PH Tang is a later car than the ones named.

## Not Recalls: Owner-Reported Faults Worth Knowing

This section is deliberately separated, because conflating the two is how misinformation spreads. Nothing below has been recalled. These are **owner-reported faults logged in the Australian market**, where BYD has sold in volume for longer than it has here and where owner-complaint data is well documented. They are **not** PH-confirmed, and they are **not** safety recalls.

| Model | Reported issue |
|---|---|
| **Seal** (2023-on) | Drivetrain vibration and faulty rear driveshafts traced to a manufacturing defect, sometimes shredding rubber seals early |
| **Sealion 6** (2024-on) | 12-volt battery drain — the BMS failing to engage the high-voltage pack to top up the starter battery; also low-beam headlight alignment set wrong at the factory |
| **Sealion 7** (2025-on) | "Power Limited" propulsion warnings needing a firmware update to clear permanently; audio system occasionally going silent, which also mutes parking sensors and safety chimes |
| **Sealion 5** (2025-on) | Driver-monitoring camera throwing false "Distracted Driving" and "Fatigue" alerts; 12V low-battery and power-limited warnings |

The **12-volt drain on the Sealion 6 is the one most worth knowing locally**, since that model sells well here and the symptom — an EV with a healthy main battery that still won't wake up — is counterintuitive enough that owners often misdiagnose it as a dead traction pack. Kung biglang ayaw mag-boot ng sasakyan mo kahit puno ang Blade battery, ang 12V muna ang i-check, hindi agad ang malaking baterya.

## How to Actually Check Your Own Car

1. **Get your VIN** — it's on the registration, the windscreen base, and the driver's door jamb.
2. **Bring it to an authorized BYD dealer** and ask them to run it against the open-campaign system. This is the only definitive check for a PH-spec unit.
3. **Ignore foreign VIN checkers.** Most query China-market or UK/EU databases and simply will not have your car. A "no results" from one of those means nothing.
4. **Ask about silent campaigns too** — service actions and software updates that aren't formal recalls but still fix something. Libre rin karamihan ng mga iyon.
5. **Keep the paperwork.** A documented recall or campaign completion helps resale, especially on an EV where buyers are already nervous about battery history.

## Verdict: What the Recall Record Actually Tells You

Four recalls in roughly two years sounds alarming until you set it against volume — BYD now sells millions of cars a year, overwhelmingly in China, and a manufacturer that size filing four public recalls is a manufacturer whose regulator is working.

The more useful reading is in the *pattern* of the defects. Three of the four were electrical or battery-adjacent: a steering controller, a motor controller, battery sealing, battery-pack consistency. That's the profile you'd expect from a company scaling electric production faster than anyone in history. None of them were traced to the **Blade battery cell** chemistry itself — the sealing recall was about the housing, not the cells.

For a Philippine buyer, the honest summary is this: **the record is clean here, but it's clean partly because our market is young.** Every BYD sold under ACMobility is a post-2023 car, which is why the older production batches keep missing us. Mas matanda ang fleet, mas totoo ang test. Worth watching, not worth panicking over.
