The **VinFast VF 5** is the model that makes the brand's case without an asterisk attached.

It was the **first VinFast sold in the Philippines**, it charges on a **Type 2 port like a normal EV** — not the [DC-only arrangement that complicates the VF 3](/blog/vinfast-vf3-pms-guide) — it carries **six airbags**, and in Philippine testing its **326 km range claim held up**, which is genuinely rare for an NEDC figure.

It has also quietly become a much better deal than it was at launch. The battery-included price has dropped **₱92,000**, and the battery subscription fee has fallen by roughly **two-thirds** while the mileage allowance went *up*. That combination did something unusual: it **reversed which option you should pick.**

This guide covers what the VF 5 costs to buy and run today, what the published specs get wrong, and the handful of quirks that no listing site mentions.

## Key Takeaways

- **₱1,099,000 with the battery, ₱949,000 on subscription** — down from **₱1,191,000 / ₱992,000** at the July 2024 launch. Aggregators still print the old figure.
- **🔴 The subscription recommendation has flipped.** At launch the fee was **₱5,800/month for 1,500 km** — a 34-month breakeven, so buying the battery was obviously right. Today it is **₱1,950 (≤2,000 km) or ₱2,900 (>2,000 km)**, pushing breakeven to **77 or 52 months**.
- **It is the only VinFast that kept a distance-tiered subscription.** Every other model went flat-rate in February 2026.
- **Type 2 charging with DC fast charge, 10–70% in about 30–33 minutes.** This is the cheapest VinFast that works on ordinary public AC chargers.
- **326 km claimed, and a PH road test found that accurate** — against the VF 3's 20–30% shortfall.
- **Six airbags plus lane-departure warning, blind-spot monitoring and rear cross-traffic alert** — but **still no crash-test rating anywhere.**
- **⚠️ VinFast PH's own VF 5 page still advertises a "7-year warranty"** despite the September 2026 up-to-10-year announcement. Get your number in writing.
- **The quirks are real:** no Park gear (Neutral plus parking brake), no telescopic steering, Android Auto but no Apple CarPlay, stiff ride *and* noticeable body roll.

## Which Model Years Does This Guide Cover? (2024–2026)

**July 20, 2024 — launch, and the start of VinFast Philippines.** The VF 5 was the brand's first model priced and opened for reservations here, at **₱1,191,000** with the battery and **₱992,000** on subscription, with a ₱5,000 refundable reservation deposit. Launch promotions were aggressive: a **₱50,000 direct discount**, freebies worth over ₱50,000, payment support of **₱69,000 or ₱99,000** depending on financing method, and a raffle of 100 discounts worth ₱95,000 each.

**February 6, 2026 — the repositioning.** VinFast cut prices across the lineup and overhauled the battery subscription. The VF 5 landed at **₱1,099,000 / ₱949,000** — **₱92,000** off the battery-included price — while the subscription fee went from **₱5,800/month capped at 1,500 km** to **₱1,950/month up to 2,000 km**, with a **₱2,900** tier above that.

**September 2026 — the warranty program.** Up to 10 years on the vehicle and up to 10 years / 200,000 km on the battery, announced for the brand. See the warranty section below for why we are not simply repeating that for this model.

**No facelift, one variant.** The Philippine car has been the **VF 5 Plus** throughout — 37.23 kWh, 134 hp. Globally VinFast also builds a lower-powered 70 kW version with a 29.6 kWh pack; that one is not sold here, so ignore overseas reviews quoting those numbers.

**What this means if you are buying used.** Two separate traps. First, **a 2024–2025 subscription car carries the ₱5,800 contract it was sold under**, not today's ₱1,950 — ask to see the agreement and whether it can be migrated, because that difference is ₱46,200 a year. Second, the launch promos were worth well over ₱150,000 to some buyers, so **what the first owner actually paid has very little to do with today's list price.** Price the car, not their receipt.

## 🔴 The Subscription Maths — And Why the Answer Reversed

VinFast sells every car at two prices: one where you own the battery, one where you rent it. The breakeven is simply the price gap divided by the monthly fee.

**At launch, buying the battery was the obvious call:**

> ₱1,191,000 − ₱992,000 = **₱199,000 gap** ÷ **₱5,800/month** = **34 months**

Under three years to breakeven, on a car most people keep five or more. The subscription was expensive money.

**Today it is the other way around:**

| Tier | Price gap | Monthly fee | Breakeven |
|---|---|---|---|
| **≤2,000 km/month** | ₱150,000 | **₱1,950** | **77 months — 6 yrs 5 mos** |
| **>2,000 km/month** | ₱150,000 | **₱2,900** | **52 months — 4 yrs 4 mos** |

**How to read that.** If you drive lightly — under 2,000 km a month, which is most private owners — the subscription takes **six and a half years** to cost you what the battery would have. Over that period VinFast also carries the risk: under the subscription it **repairs or replaces the pack if capacity falls below 70%**, which is the single most expensive failure an EV can have.

If you drive more than 2,000 km a month, the higher tier shortens breakeven to **four years and four months** — still longer than the launch car's 34 months, but close enough that a long-term keeper should buy the battery outright.

<Pullquote>The car didn't change. The contract did — and it moved the right answer from "buy the battery" to "it depends how far you drive."</Pullquote>

**The VF 5 is the only VinFast that still has distance tiers.** Every other model in the range went to a flat unlimited-distance fee in February 2026. We work through the breakeven for all eight priced variants — they range from 62 months on the VF 3 to over 33 years on the VF 9 — in our [full VinFast Philippines price and ownership breakdown](/blog/vinfast-philippines-price-list-guide).

## Charging: The Part the VF 3 Gets Wrong and This Car Gets Right

If you have read our [VF 3 guide](/blog/vinfast-vf3-pms-guide), you know the cheapest VinFast has a real constraint: **DC charging only, no AC inlet**, confirmed by two Philippine publications that drove it. Ordinary AC public chargers — the cheap, common kind here — simply cannot be used.

**The VF 5 does not have that problem.** AutoDeal's road test lists it with a **Type 2 charger**, alongside DC fast charging that takes it from **10% to 70% in about 30 minutes** (VinFast's own page says under 33; one owner reports 34). A full AC charge takes around **six hours**, which is an ordinary overnight.

**What that unlocks in practice:**

- **A standard home wallbox works.** You are buying the same equipment any BYD or Hyundai owner buys.
- **Mall and office AC chargers are usable**, which is most of the public network outside the DC hubs.
- **V-Green DC stations are free until March 31, 2029** — roughly 137 locations across 22 areas — so the fast option is also the cheapest one.

**One thing still to verify yourself.** VinFast Philippines does not state the **DC standard** on the VF 5's model page. Type 2 on the AC side plus the Philippine network's standardisation on CCS2 makes CCS2 the overwhelmingly likely answer, but we are not going to print a connector spec we cannot source from the manufacturer. **Photograph the port and show it to whoever sells you charging equipment** — the same rule we applied to [early BYD Dolphins sold here with GB/T connectors](/blog/byd-dolphin-pms-guide).

## Range: The Claim That Actually Held Up

This deserves its own section because it is unusual.

NEDC range figures are optimistic almost by design. The VF 3 claims 210–215 km and returns **150–170 km** with the aircon on. BYD's Dolphin claims 405 km NEDC and returned about **300 km** in Philippine testing.

**The VF 5 claims 326 km, and AutoDeal's road test — run across mixed speeds and traffic density — found that figure accurate.**

Two honest caveats. That is **one road test, not a controlled comparison**, and conditions vary enormously with aircon load, traffic and terrain. And "accurate" in a reviewer's hands is not a guarantee for your commute up Aguinaldo Highway with four adults aboard.

But it is the best evidence available, and it lands the VF 5 in an interesting spot: **on observed numbers it sits very close to a BYD Dolphin that costs ₱339,000 more.** Different tests, different outlets, so treat it as a strong signal rather than a measurement — but it is the strongest thing the VF 5 has going for it.

**What 326 km buys you:** a week of Metro Manila commuting on one charge for most people, a Manila–Baguio run with one planned DC stop, and genuine freedom from the range arithmetic that dominates VF 3 ownership. Combined with a **150 km/h top speed**, this is the first VinFast in the range that is comfortable on an expressway.

## Maintenance: Still No Price List

**VinFast Philippines publishes no PMS schedule or price list for the VF 5**, exactly as with the VF 3. We looked, we did not find one, and we are not going to assemble a cost table out of assumptions.

What we can tell you:

- **The cadence is EV cadence** — annual or roughly 20,000 km. Tyres, brake fluid, cabin filter, battery thermal circuit, suspension, software. Do not carry over the [light-versus-heavy split that structures a petrol car's service life](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide).
- **Regenerative braking means the friction brakes do very little work.** Expect long pad life.
- **The 12V battery remains the most common stranding cause** on any EV. Replace it early rather than late.

**And the service network is structurally different from BYD's or Toyota's.** VinFast's Philippine partners are certified **third-party workshops** — Sun Master, Brilliant Car Solutions, **Bosch Service**, SMS Tyre Depot, Iconique, **Car Doctor, Auto Casa**, HH Asia Tyre — with a Motech agreement targeting up to **63 workshops**. Coverage scales quickly that way, but EV depth at any individual branch is unproven. Our [guide to when the dealer premium is worth paying](/blog/casa-vs-talyer-philippines) assumes a casa exists; here the right question is **which branch handles your warranty work and whether it has an EV-trained technician.**

## ⚠️ Warranty: Check This One Yourself

The September 2026 program says **up to 10 years** on the vehicle and **up to 10 years / 200,000 km** on the battery, with a Residual Value Guarantee of up to 90% of original net retail after six months, tapering to 70% after three years.

**But VinFast Philippines' own VF 5 model page still advertises a "7-year warranty"** as of this writing — and the launch material quoted **7 years or 160,000 km, whichever comes first.**

We are not calling that a contradiction in bad faith. "**Up to** 10 years" was always doing work, and no model-by-model breakdown has been published. But it does mean **you cannot assume the headline applies to this car.** Ask for the specific term for your VIN, in writing, before you sign — and treat any salesperson who quotes the ten-year figure without producing paperwork as someone repeating a press release.

Under the battery subscription, battery coverage is a separate matter entirely: VinFast maintains, repairs and replaces the pack throughout, including **replacement if capacity drops below 70%**.

## The Quirks Nobody Lists

These come from a hands-on Philippine road test, and none of them appear on a spec sheet:

- **🔴 There is no Park gear.** You use **Neutral plus the parking brake**. If you have driven automatics your whole life, this is a habit you must consciously build — especially on a slope, where the parking brake is doing a job you are used to a transmission doing. Make the check part of getting out of the car.
- **No telescopic steering adjustment.** Taller drivers will struggle to find a good position; the wheel moves up and down but not toward you.
- **Android Auto but no Apple CarPlay.** Promised in a future update. Launch material claimed both — if CarPlay matters, confirm it on the actual unit rather than the brochure.
- **Stiff suspension *and* noticeable body roll.** An unusual pairing, and a fair description: it fidgets over broken surfaces without feeling planted through corners. Steering is responsive but short on feedback.
- **The back seat realistically fits two.** Rear legroom and headroom are limited for a car of this footprint; treat the third rear belt as an occasional-use item.

### On Safety: Six Airbags, Still No Rating

The VF 5's equipment is a genuine step up from the VF 3: **six airbags** (front, side, curtain), ABS, EBD, brake assist, stability control, **lane-departure warning, blind-spot monitoring, rear cross-traffic alert**, rear sensors and camera, and **ISOFIX** mounts. Launch material also cites adaptive cruise control and emergency braking.

**But there is still no crash-test rating.** Not ASEAN NCAP, not Euro NCAP, not ANCAP. VinFast's **VF 6 and VF 7 earned 5-star ratings from India's Bharat NCAP**, and the older VF e34 holds a **4-star ASEAN NCAP** result — neither of which tells you anything about this car, under a different protocol in a different market.

Six airbags and a full ADAS suite are real, measurable equipment. They are not a crash result. For a car that will carry your family, that distinction is worth holding onto.

## Known Issues

The VF 5 has been on sale here since July 2024 — long enough for first impressions, not long enough for a reliability record. **We found no verified widespread defect pattern in the Philippine fleet, and we will not invent one.**

What exists:

- **Philippine owner sentiment is positive but thin** — six owner reviews averaging **4.17/5** on one major listing site, praising the drive, the cabin and the fast charging. Six reviews is a signal, not a sample, and none of them covers long-term reliability, warranty service or maintenance costs.
- **After-sales responsiveness is the brand-level theme** in the complaints we found across VinFast's Philippine lineup — templated replies, unclear timelines, thin parts stocking. That is the risk to plan around, more than mechanical failure.
- **Ride quality and the missing CarPlay** are the consistent reviewer criticisms, and both are characteristics rather than faults.
- **From other markets, as context only:** VinFast recalled over 6,000 **VF 8** units in the US for a steering software defect, and US regulators opened an investigation into VF 8 lane-keep assist complaints. **The VF 8 is not sold in the Philippines** and none of it touches the VF 5 — but it is fair evidence of a young company still refining its software and processes in public.

## Verdict: The VinFast to Buy if You Only Buy One

The VF 5 is the point in this lineup where the compromises stop being structural and start being ordinary.

It charges on a standard Type 2 port. It seats five through four doors. It has six airbags and a real ADAS suite. It will cruise at expressway speed. Its range claim survived contact with a Philippine road test, which is more than most EVs manage. And at **₱1,099,000** — ₱92,000 less than it cost at launch — it undercuts the BYD Dolphin by ₱339,000 while landing remarkably close to it on observed range.

**Three things to settle before you buy:**

1. **Get the warranty term in writing.** VinFast's own VF 5 page still says seven years while the brand announces ten. One of those is the number on your contract, and you should know which.
2. **Decide the battery question deliberately.** Under 2,000 km a month and keeping the car a decade? Buy the battery. Driving more, or planning to change cars in four years? The subscription now makes sense in a way it did not in 2024.
3. **Accept the quirks on a test drive, not on paper.** The missing Park gear and the fixed-reach steering wheel are small things that you will either stop noticing in a week or resent for years. Twenty minutes in the car tells you which.

Get those three right and this is the most sensible VinFast on sale in the Philippines — and a genuinely strong EV at the money, asterisks and all.
