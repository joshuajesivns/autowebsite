The **BYD Sealion 6 DM-i** is the model that quietly did the heavy lifting for BYD Philippines — a compact PHEV SUV that has been a top performer two years running, and the car most likely to be cross-shopped against a Toyota Corolla Cross or a Ford Territory by a buyer who is EV-curious but not ready to give up a gas tank. It launched here in **September 2024** under **ACMobility**, and it is the companion piece to our [BYD sales ranking breakdown](/blog/top-selling-byd-cars-philippines-guide) and our [general PMS cost guide](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide).

Here is the part that makes this guide different from the [Seal 5 writeup](/blog/byd-seal-5-pms-guide), where we had to admit no PMS pricing existed: **for the Sealion 6, real per-interval service costs do exist.** They came from a BYD dealership and were verified inside ACMobility. We can finally put actual pesos on a BYD PHEV service book.

## Key Takeaways

- **One variant only.** Despite what some listing sites imply, the Sealion 6 DM-i has been a **single-spec model** since launch — no Dynamic, no Premium, no trim ladder.
- **Current SRP: ₱1,598,000**, up ₱50,000 from the ₱1,548,000 launch price. The increase took effect **May 7, 2026** — aggregators still showing ₱1,548,000 are stale.
- **Full 5-year / 100,000 km PMS cost: ₱48,453.06**, across only **six** service visits. Dealer-sourced, ACMobility-verified — not an official published price list.
- **Budget for the 80,000 km stop (₱14,399.90).** It is more than double the visits on either side of it.
- **Warranty is three separate numbers:** 6 yr / 150,000 km vehicle, 8 yr / 150,000 km motor, 8 yr / 160,000 km Blade battery.
- **A 2027 split into Dynamic and Premium** has appeared on the DOE database with more range and a full ADAS suite — but no launch date and no price.
- **Its 5-star rating is ANCAP and Euro NCAP, not ASEAN NCAP.** Do not compare its score against the ASEAN NCAP numbers on our other guides.

## Which Model Year Does This Guide Cover? (2024–2026)

This guide covers the Sealion 6 DM-i from its **September 2024** Philippine launch through the **2026** model year. Across that whole run it has been sold as **one unnamed variant** — same 18.3 kWh battery, same powertrain, same equipment list. There is no trim decision to agonise over, which is unusual for a car at this price.

**What did change is the price.** It launched at **₱1,548,000** and stayed there for roughly eighteen months. On **May 7, 2026**, BYD Cars Philippines quietly listed it at **₱1,598,000** — a **₱50,000 increase** with no accompanying spec change announced. This matters when you shop: several aggregator sites still print the old ₱1,548,000 figure, so a quote that looks ₱50,000 cheaper than the official site is probably just an un-updated listing, not a deal.

**And a bigger change is queued.** In **July 2026**, two Sealion 6 DM-i trims — a **Dynamic** and a **Premium** — appeared on the Department of Energy's database, which is typically where PH-market variants surface before an official announcement. We cover what is in them further down. As of this writing there is **no confirmed launch date and no pricing** from BYD Philippines for either.

## The PMS Schedule: Actual Numbers, Finally

Most BYD PH models have no published service pricing. The Sealion 6 is the exception. The figures below were obtained from a **specific BYD dealership** and independently **verified as accurate by sources inside ACMobility**, the distributor — reported by CarGuide.PH in **April 2025**.

|---|---|---|

| 2nd service | 12 months / 20,000 km | ₱6,372.53 |
| 3rd service | 24 months / 40,000 km | ₱7,584.97 |
| 4th service | 36 months / 60,000 km | ₱8,600.25 |
| 5th service | **48 months / 80,000 km** | **₱14,399.90** |

**Three honest caveats before you budget around this.** First, it is **not an official BYD Philippines price list** — it is one dealership's sheet, corroborated internally, which is stronger than owner hearsay but weaker than a published document. Second, the figures are from **April 2025**, now around seventeen months old; parts and labour rates move, and the car itself got a ₱50,000 price increase in that window. Third, the source **does not itemise what is included** at each stop, so we cannot tell you exactly which fluids or filters buy you that 80,000 km spike.

**The 80,000 km visit is the one to save for.** At **₱14,399.90** it is roughly *double* the services on either side of it, and it lands in year four — right when an owner has settled into assuming this car is cheap to keep. Set money aside for it early.

## Six Visits in 100,000 km — Why the Cadence Is the Real Saving

The headline is not any single service price. It is **how rarely you go**.

After the first 3-month/5,000 km check, the Sealion 6 moves to a **once-a-year or every-20,000 km** rhythm. Over 100,000 km that is **six visits total**. A conventional petrol crossover on a 5,000 km interval would be at fifteen or twenty visits over the same distance; even a modern 10,000 km-interval hybrid like the [Toyota Corolla Cross](/blog/toyota-corolla-cross-pms-guide) is at ten.

Every visit you skip is not just the invoice — it is the half-day, the drop-off, the queue, the shuttle back to the office. For a Calabarzon or Metro Manila owner, that is the part that actually changes your year.

**Do not, however, read "PHEV" as "almost no maintenance."** This car still has a 1.5L petrol engine with oil, an oil filter, an air filter, coolant and a 12V battery. That is precisely why it costs what it does — and why it is not in the same league as a pure BEV, which we can now show directly.

## PHEV vs. BEV: What the Engine Actually Costs You

Our [BYD Seagull guide](/blog/byd-seagull-pms-guide) puts a comparable BYD **battery-electric** model's servicing at roughly **₱21,271.73 cumulative over five years**. The Sealion 6 DM-i comes in at **₱48,453.06** over the same period.

**The plug-in hybrid costs roughly 2.3× the pure EV to maintain.** The gap is the engine — oil changes, filters, spark plugs, a cooling system, an exhaust. A PHEV is genuinely two powertrains, and you service both.

That is not an argument against the Sealion 6. It is an argument for being clear-eyed about what you are buying: the Sealion 6 sells you **range security** — over 1,100 km combined, no charging anxiety on a Bicol run — and the ~₱27,000 five-year difference is what that security costs on the service side. If your driving genuinely never leaves a 100 km radius, a BEV is cheaper in every direction and you should know that before you sign.

**Read that comparison carefully.** The Sealion's number is an exact dealer quote; the Corolla Cross column is our own *estimated range* built from its light/heavy service bands, because Toyota PH publishes no model-specific price list either. Comparing a precise figure to an estimate always flatters the precise one. The defensible claim is the **visit count** — six versus ten is a documented interval difference, not an estimate.

Then there is fuel. Charge at home nightly and the daily commute runs largely on residential electricity rather than pump prices; the engine becomes the thing you use for provincial trips. That is where a PHEV's real running-cost case is won — but only if you can actually plug it in, which brings us to the honest part.

## The PH Charging Reality — And Why It Matters Less Here

The Sealion 6 accepts **6.6 kW AC** charging (a full charge in roughly three hours) and **18 kW DC** fast charging (30–80% in about 35 minutes). *These figures come from spec aggregators rather than BYD PH's own documentation — worth confirming at the dealer.*

**18 kW is slow by modern EV standards**, and for once that barely matters. The pack is only 18.3 kWh — a fraction of what a long-range BEV carries — so a public DC session saves you minutes, not hours, and you would only ever need one in an edge case. The Sealion 6's charging story is **overnight AC at home**, full stop.

Which sets up the real question for a Philippine buyer: **saan ka magcha-charge?** If you have a garage or a dedicated slot with an outlet, this car makes sense immediately — you wake up every day with a full 105 km of electric range and the engine sits unused for weeks. If you are in a condo where the parking has no power, or you street-park, you will end up running it as an ordinary and rather heavy 1,830 kg hybrid, paying PHEV money for a fraction of the benefit.

Be brutally honest with yourself about your parking before this car makes financial sense. That single question decides more of your ownership cost than anything on the spec sheet.

## Warranty: Three Different Numbers

BYD Philippines coverage splits three ways, and people routinely quote only the first:

| Component | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Whole vehicle | **6 years / 150,000 km** |
| Electric motor | **8 years / 150,000 km** |
| Blade (LFP) battery | **8 years / 160,000 km** |

**Do not import the European numbers.** In January 2026 BYD extended its Blade battery warranty to **8 years / 250,000 km** — that applies to **Europe**, not the Philippines. If you see 250,000 km quoted locally, it is someone reading an overseas press release.

This coverage is the quiet argument for the car. Six years of whole-vehicle warranty is longer than most PH mainstream brands offer, and against that backdrop a ₱48,453 five-year service bill looks less like a cost and more like the price of keeping that coverage valid.

## Safety: 5 Stars — But Not the Stars You Think

The Sealion 6 DM-i holds **5-star ratings from both ANCAP (Australasia) and Euro NCAP**.

**⚠️ Those are not ASEAN NCAP results, and the scores are not comparable to the ones in our other guides.** Several of our comparison posts — the [Xpander vs. Veloz vs. BR-V three-way](/blog/xpander-vs-veloz-vs-br-v), for instance — quote ASEAN NCAP overall scores out of 100 under a specific regional protocol. ANCAP and Euro NCAP use **different tests, different weightings and different denominators**. Putting the Sealion's five stars beside a Veloz's "79.99" tells you nothing useful. Stars from different programmes are different currencies.

What is fair to say: it has been independently crash-tested to a five-star standard by two credible programmes, which is more third-party validation than a number of cars in its price bracket can show.

## Known Issues on the BYD Sealion 6

**No Philippine-specific recall for the Sealion 6 could be found.** The widely reported BYD recall of roughly **110,000–115,000 units** covered the **Tang and Yuan Pro** in China — **not** this model, and neither of those is sold here in volume.

The car has now been on sale locally for **two years**, so it has more road time than the Seal 5 did when we wrote that guide. Even so, **we could not verify a widespread defect pattern on PH units**, and we are not going to invent one to fill the section. As with every young model we cover, we would rather say "not yet established" than manufacture a scare.

**What reviewers do consistently criticise is dynamics, not reliability.** Road tests flag noticeable **body roll**, **suspension tuned very soft**, and **light steering that needs regular correction at expressway speeds**. That is a tuning character, not a fault — but if you are coming from something firmer, drive it on the expressway before you commit, not just around the village.

Two things we would watch as the fleet ages: how the **12V battery** holds up (a common weak point across PHEVs and EVs generally, and not covered by the glamorous 8-year traction battery warranty), and whether the **engine's low duty cycle** — it can genuinely sit unused for weeks in a home-charging household — creates any long-term fuel-system or oil-condition issues. Neither is a reported problem. Both are worth asking your service adviser about at the 20,000 km visit.

## Should You Wait for the 2027 Dynamic and Premium?

The DOE filing from July 2026 tells us the shape of the update:

- **Both trims** keep the 18.3 kWh Blade battery and the 1.5L generator engine, rated at **214 hp / 300 Nm**, with combined range up to **1,200 km** — about **100 km more** than the current car.
- **Premium** adds the **DiPilot** advanced driver-assist suite, a **power tailgate**, **ambient lighting**, and a **wireless charging pad**.
- **Dynamic** goes without those to hit a lower price.

There is a small tell that this is close: BYD PH's current model page already lists three colours — Stone Grey, Harbour Grey and Arctic White — marked **"Premium only"**, on a car that does not yet have a Premium trim.

There is also a wider signal. The Sealion 6 is sold in China as the **Song Plus**, and BYD **ended its domestic Chinese sales in January 2026** with a replacement taking shape, while global sales continue. The nameplate has moved more than **1.5 million units** worldwide since 2020. Read together with a two-trim 2027 filing, that points to a generational refresh rather than a wind-down — and BYD PH raising the local price in May 2026 is not the behaviour of a distributor clearing out a dead model.

**Our take:** if you want **DiPilot**, wait — this is the first Sealion 6 that will offer a full ADAS suite, and that is a genuine capability gap, not a trim upgrade. If you do not, the current car is the known quantity: one spec, a settled price, and the only BYD PHEV here with a **dealer-verified service schedule attached to it**. The 2027 cars will arrive with no PMS pricing at all, and going by BYD PH's track record, it may be a year before anyone can tell you what they cost to run.

## Dealer Network & Service Access

ACMobility has grown BYD's dealer footprint aggressively — from **2 outlets to 79** over the brand's expansion here — which was the single biggest practical worry about buying a Chinese EV in the Philippines a few years ago. For South Luzon owners in particular, casa access is no longer the obstacle it once was.

That said, a PHEV is a specialist repair. Unlike a Vios or a Ranger, you cannot take this to any competent neighbourhood shop when something electrical goes wrong. Our [casa vs. talyer breakdown](/blog/casa-vs-talyer-philippines) explains where the line falls generally — for the Sealion 6, the line sits much further toward the casa than it would on a petrol car, and staying inside the casa network is also what protects that six-year warranty.

## What You Can DIY

Less than you would think, and less than you should attempt.

**Reasonable:** cabin air filter, wiper blades, tyre pressures, 12V battery terminal checks, keeping the charging port and cable clean and dry.

**Leave it alone:** anything orange-cabled. The high-voltage system is not a place for improvisation, and unauthorised work there is the fastest way to void the 8-year battery coverage. Engine oil changes are technically ordinary, but on a 20,000 km interval with warranty coverage on the line, the labour saving is not worth the paperwork risk.

## Verdict: The BYD That Finally Shows Its Numbers

The Sealion 6 DM-i has been BYD Philippines' quiet workhorse, and it is now the model we can write about most confidently — because for once the service costs are on the table instead of behind a "confirm with your dealer."

**₱48,453.06 over five years and 100,000 km, across six visits**, with a six-year vehicle warranty and an eight-year battery behind it, is a genuinely competitive ownership case against any petrol crossover in the ₱1.5–1.6M bracket. The caveats are real — that figure is a dealership sheet rather than a published price list, it is seventeen months old, and the 80,000 km stop will sting.

The decision, though, rarely comes down to the service book. It comes down to **whether you can plug this car in at home**. If you can, it is one of the most sensible things on sale here at the price. If you cannot, you are buying an expensive, heavy hybrid and leaving its best feature in the box.
