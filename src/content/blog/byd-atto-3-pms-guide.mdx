The **BYD Atto 3** is the car that made BYD a real brand in the Philippines rather than a curiosity — the first BYD most Filipinos actually saw on the road, and still the nameplate people picture when they hear "BYD." It launched here on **November 17, 2023** under what was then AC Motors, now **ACMobility**, and it was 2024's best-selling pure electric BYD at **1,062 units**, roughly 45% of the brand's BEV sales that year, as we covered in our [ranking of BYD's volume sellers](/blog/top-selling-byd-cars-philippines-guide).

Here is why this guide matters more than most: the Atto 3 is one of only **three BYD models in the Philippines with real, per-visit service pricing on record**. Our [Seagull writeup](/blog/byd-seagull-pms-guide) had to borrow the Atto 3's figures as a proxy because no Seagull-specific list exists. This is the post where those numbers actually come from — itemised, with the caveats attached.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five-year, 100,000 km servicing: ₱21,271.73** across six visits — an average of **₱4,254.35 a year**. Dealer-sourced and verified inside ACMobility, but not an official BYD Philippines price list.
- **Three of the six visits cost exactly ₱2,400.** The whole schedule is two real bills (40,000 and 80,000 km) and four cheap ones.
- **The price went up on May 7, 2026.** Dynamic is now **₱1,638,000** (+₱40,000), Premium **₱1,848,000** (+₱50,000). Listing sites still showing ₱1,598,000 / ₱1,798,000 are stale, not cheap.
- **It costs less than half the [Sealion 6 DM-i](/blog/byd-sealion-6-pms-guide) to service** — ₱21,271.73 against ₱48,453.06. That gap is what a petrol engine costs you.
- **The 2025 facelift was free.** Same money, bigger screen, power tailgate, better cabin — which makes a 2025-or-later used unit the one to hunt for.
- **Warranty is three separate numbers:** 6 yr / 150,000 km vehicle, 8 yr / 150,000 km motor, 8 yr / 160,000 km Blade battery.
- **The PH car is now the old one globally.** An 800V Atto 3 Evo is on sale in Europe and an all-new, longer Atto 3 broke cover at Auto China in April 2026. Neither is confirmed here.

## Which Model Year Does This Guide Cover? (2023–2026)

Three distinct phases, and the difference between them matters if you are shopping used.

**November 2023 – January 2025 (pre-facelift).** The Atto 3 arrived on **November 17, 2023** in two variants at **₱1,598,000** (Dynamic, 410 km NEDC) and **₱1,798,000** (Premium, 480 km NEDC). Mechanically this is the same car sold today.

**January 20, 2025 (facelift).** BYD Philippines refreshed the Atto 3 and — this is the part worth noting — **kept both prices exactly the same**. The Premium gained a **15.6-inch rotating touchscreen** in place of the 12.8-inch unit and a **powered tailgate**; both variants got ambient lighting through the cabin, new interior colourways, **NFC card keys**, 18-inch wheels, and a rear badge that now reads a simple "BYD" instead of the old "Build Your Dreams" script. Battery, range, motor output and platform carried over unchanged.

**Read that as a used-buyer signal.** A 2025-or-later unit is a materially better-equipped car than a 2023–2024 one that originally cost the same money. When two listings are close in price and mileage, the facelift is the tiebreaker — check the screen size and the rear badge, they are the fastest way to tell them apart in photos.

**May 7, 2026 (the price increase).** BYD Cars Philippines' own model pages now list the Dynamic at **₱1,638,000** and the Premium at **₱1,848,000** — **₱40,000 and ₱50,000 up** respectively, with no spec change announced. The official price list carries these figures as valid **as of July 28, 2026**, so they are current, not a typo.

**⚠️ This is where shopping gets confusing.** Several aggregator and listing sites still print ₱1,598,000 and ₱1,798,000. If a quote looks ₱50,000 below BYD's own site, check the date on the page before you get excited — it is almost always an un-updated listing rather than a discount. The same thing happened to the Sealion 6 after its identical **May 7, 2026** increase, so this looks like a lineup-wide adjustment rather than an Atto 3 quirk.

## The PMS Schedule: What Six Visits Actually Cost

The figures below come from a **specific BYD dealership**, independently **verified as accurate by sources inside ACMobility**, the distributor — reported by CarGuide.PH on **April 13, 2025**. It is the same reporting that gave us the Sealion 6's numbers.

| Interval | When | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1st service | 3 months / 5,000 km | ₱2,240.00 |
| 2nd service | 12 months / 20,000 km | ₱2,400.00 |
| 3rd service | **24 months / 40,000 km** | **₱5,196.14** |
| 4th service | 36 months / 60,000 km | ₱2,400.00 |
| 5th service | **48 months / 80,000 km** | **₱6,633.59** |
| 6th service | 60 months / 100,000 km | ₱2,400.00 |
| **5-year total** | **100,000 km** | **₱21,271.73** |

**The shape of that table is the story.** Four of the six visits are routine inspections in the ₱2,240–2,400 range — three of them priced identically at **₱2,400.00**. The money is concentrated in **two** stops: 40,000 km and 80,000 km, which together account for **₱11,829.73**, or roughly **56% of five years of servicing**. Budget for those two and the rest is close to noise.

After 100,000 km the schedule **resets** — it skips the 3-month/5,000 km first service and simply resumes the 20,000 km rhythm.

**Three honest caveats.** First, this is **one dealership's sheet**, corroborated internally at ACMobility — stronger than owner hearsay, weaker than a published document, and BYD PH still does not publish model-specific service pricing. Second, the figures are from **April 2025**, now around seventeen months old, and the car itself has had a price increase since. Third, the source **does not itemise what is included** at each visit, so we cannot tell you which specific fluids buy you the 40,000 and 80,000 km bills.

**One small thing we are flagging rather than quietly fixing:** the six line items add up to **₱21,269.73**, while the published cumulative total is **₱21,271.73** — a **₱2.00** discrepancy in the source. It changes nothing about your budget, but we would rather point at it than round it away in silence.

## What "PMS" Even Means on a BEV

There is no engine oil, no oil filter, no spark plugs, no timing belt, no fuel filter, no exhaust. Strip those out and a service visit becomes mostly inspection plus a short list of genuine wear items:

- **Brake fluid** — ages on a calendar, not on mileage, and regenerative braking means the pads themselves last far longer than you are used to
- **Battery and motor coolant** — the Blade pack and the drive unit are liquid-cooled
- **Cabin air filter** — the one filter you still change often, and worth doing on schedule in PH traffic
- **Tyres** — instant torque plus battery weight wears fronts faster than on a comparable petrol crossover
- **12V auxiliary battery** — the humble part that strands more EVs than the traction battery ever will
- **Brake calipers** — precisely *because* the pads are barely used, sliders and pistons can seize from disuse in our humidity; this is a real EV failure mode, not a theoretical one

**Hindi ito "PMS" sa dating sense.** After the first check at 3 months, you go **once a year or every 20,000 km**. Over 100,000 km that is **six visits**, against fifteen to twenty for a petrol crossover on a 5,000 km interval — and even a modern 10,000 km hybrid like the [Corolla Cross and its own service schedule](/blog/toyota-corolla-cross-pms-guide) is at ten. The saving is not only the invoices; it is the half-days you do not spend at a casa.

## The PH Charging Reality — And the 3.3 kW Bonus

**7 kW AC is the number that matters.** On a proper Type 2 wall box the Atto 3 fills in about **8 to 8.5 hours** — an overnight car, exactly as intended. A **2.3 kW portable charger** comes in the boot for emergencies; treat it as a trickle for a car you forgot to plug in, not a charging plan, because at that rate a full charge is a weekend.

DC fast charging is the edge case. Whether the real ceiling is 70, 80 or 88 kW, you are looking at a coffee-length stop rather than a fifteen-minute splash, and the PH public network — while much better than it was in 2023 — is still concentrated along expressway corridors and a handful of malls. As we found in our [look at South Luzon's EV shift](/blog/the-silent-surge-south-luzon-ev-era), the region's horizontal subdivisions are the real unlock: a garage with a 220V line beats any public charger.

**Saan ka magcha-charge?** Answer that before anything on the spec sheet. Garage or dedicated slot with power, and this car makes immediate sense — you start every morning at 100% and never see a fuel station. Condo parking with no outlet, or street parking, and a 480 km battery becomes a logistics problem you will resent within a month.

**Then there is V2L, which is genuinely useful here.** The Atto 3 puts out **3.3 kW** through vehicle-to-load — enough for lights, fans, a fridge, routers, laptops and phone charging during a brownout. It is not a whole-home backup and BYD does not sell it as one, but in a country that loses power to typhoons on a schedule, a car that doubles as a generator you already own is not a gimmick. Ask the dealer whether the V2L adapter is included with your unit rather than assuming it is in the boot.

|---|---|
| Whole vehicle | **6 years / 150,000 km** |
| Electric motor | **8 years / 150,000 km** |

Six years of whole-vehicle cover is longer than most mainstream PH brands offer, and against that a ₱21,271.73 five-year service bill starts to look like the cheapest part of ownership.

**Do not import the European numbers.** BYD extended its Blade battery warranty to **8 years / 250,000 km** in January 2026 — that is **Europe**, not the Philippines. Anyone quoting 250,000 km locally is reading an overseas press release.

## Known Issues on the BYD Atto 3

**The recall you will find if you search — and what it actually covers.** On **September 30, 2024**, BYD recalled **96,714** vehicles in China: 87,762 Dolphin and Yuan Plus units built between **February 4 and December 26, 2023**, plus 8,952 Yuan Plus units built between **November 2, 2022 and June 19, 2023**. The defect was in the **electric power steering column (CEPS) controller** — fitting the plastic cover created micro-cracks in a capacitor, which could grow into a short circuit, overheat and burn. The fix was a free insulation gasket.

**Yuan Plus is the Atto 3's Chinese name, so this is our car.** ACMobility's position is explicit: **units sold in the Philippines are not affected.** That is a distributor statement rather than an independent audit, but it is on the record, and the build-date windows largely predate the PH launch.

**🔴 One naming trap worth burning into memory.** The Atto 3 is the **Yuan Plus**. The *other* widely reported BYD recall — roughly 110,000–115,000 units — covered the **Tang and the Yuan Pro**, which is a different, smaller model. Several outlets have conflated Yuan Pro with the Atto 3. They are not the same car, and that recall has nothing to do with this one.

**What owners actually complain about** (mostly Australian and European fleets, where the Atto 3 has more road years than it does here — we could not verify a PH-specific defect pattern, and we are not going to invent one):

- **12V battery going flat**, leaving the car dead and needing a jump or a tow. This is the single most reported Atto 3 fault on 2022–2024 units, and it is the one to ask your service adviser about. It is also not covered by the glamorous 8-year traction battery warranty.
- **Over-eager driver assistance** — phantom braking, jerky lane-keep, intrusive steering correction, typically triggered by shadows, roadside vegetation or oncoming traffic on curves. Euro NCAP's 74% safety-assist score is the same story told in points. Learn where the off switches are, and drive it on a real road before you commit.
- **Minor cabin gremlins** — a sunshade that sticks open, a radio that switches itself on after parking (owners suspect the 12V again).

None of these is a safety recall in this market. All of them are things to raise at the 20,000 km visit rather than discover on the North Luzon Expressway.

## Should You Wait for the New One?

This is the most uncomfortable section in the guide, and skipping it would be dishonest.

**The Atto 3 sold here is the 2023 design.** Globally it has already been superseded twice over:

- **Atto 3 Evo** — on sale in Europe since **February 2026**. Moves to an **800V architecture**, a **74.88 kWh** LFP pack, **up to 220 kW DC** and **11 kW AC** charging, around **510 km WLTP**, an AWD option, and a 15.6-inch screen paired with an 8.8-inch driver's display.
- **An all-new Atto 3** — shown at **Auto China in April 2026**. Nearly **200 mm longer** at 4,665 mm, with **57.545 kWh / 540 km** and **68.547 kWh / 630 km** battery options, single-motor RWD around 268 hp or dual-motor around 322 hp, and "flash charging" BYD claims takes it from 10% to 97% in **nine minutes**.

**Neither is confirmed for the Philippines**, and BYD PH has given no hint of updating the Atto 3 — the Sealion 7 and Atto 2 are ahead of it in the local queue. So the realistic choice is not "wait six months for the 800V car." It is: buy the known quantity now, or wait an unknown length of time for something with no PH price, no PH launch date, and — going by BYD PH's record — no service pricing for a year after it arrives.

**Our take:** the car on sale here is three years into its run, which is exactly why it is the one with **verified service costs, a settled parts supply, 79 dealers and a documented reliability picture**. That is worth something real. But go in clear-eyed: you are buying the outgoing global model at a price that went *up* in May 2026, and its replacement will make it look dated the moment it lands. If resale value in year four is central to your decision, that is the risk, and no one can price it for you yet.

## Dealer Network & Service Access

ACMobility took BYD from **2 outlets in early 2024 to 79 by February 2026** — the single biggest change in the practical case for buying a Chinese EV here. For most of Luzon, casa access is no longer the objection it was when the Atto 3 launched.

That reach matters more on an EV than on a Vios. Our [rundown of which jobs are worth the dealer premium](/blog/casa-vs-talyer-philippines) maps where the line generally falls; for the Atto 3 it sits much further toward the casa, because almost nothing under the bonnet is serviceable by a neighbourhood shop and unauthorised high-voltage work is the fastest route to voiding eight years of battery coverage.

## What You Can DIY

**Reasonable:** cabin air filter, wiper blades, tyre pressures and rotation, washer fluid, 12V battery terminal checks, keeping the charge port and cable clean and dry.

**Leave it alone:** anything orange-cabled. The high-voltage system is not a place to improvise, and the warranty exposure is enormous relative to the labour you would save. Even brake work is worth handing over — on a regen-braked car the pads outlast several sets of expectations, but the calipers need someone who knows to check them for seizure.

## Verdict: The Cheapest BYD to Keep, at a Moment of Awkward Timing

On running costs the Atto 3 makes an easy case. **₱21,271.73 over five years and 100,000 km, six visits, four of them under ₱2,500**, with a six-year vehicle warranty and an eight-year battery behind it — that is less than half what BYD's own plug-in hybrid costs to service, and a fraction of any petrol crossover in the bracket. Add 3.3 kW of V2L for brownout season and the ownership story is genuinely strong.

The complication is timing, not the car. You are buying a 2023-architecture EV that got **more expensive in May 2026** while its global replacement is already on sale elsewhere. That is a fair trade if you value a settled, documented, well-supported machine over the newest silicon — and a bad one if you will spend year three reading about the 800V version you did not get.

As with every EV we cover, though, the decision usually comes down to one thing: **can you plug it in at home?** If yes, this is one of the most sensible things on sale at the price. If no, no service schedule in the world will make it work for you.
