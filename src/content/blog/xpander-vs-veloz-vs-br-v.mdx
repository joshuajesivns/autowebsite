Two of the three best-selling vehicles in the Philippines are seven-seat MPVs. That's not a trend piece — that's CAMPI's 2025 numbers, where the **Mitsubishi Xpander finished number one overall with 28,081 units**, ahead of the Vios, with the Avanza third. Kung nagtataka ka kung bakit puro MPV ang nakikita mo sa kalsada, iyan ang sagot.

So if you're shopping this segment, the shortlist is usually three: the **Xpander**, the **Toyota Veloz**, and the **Honda BR-V**. They look interchangeable on a spec sheet — 1.5-litre gas, front-wheel drive, seven seats, timing chain, all landing within about ₱15,000 of each other at the entry point. They are not interchangeable to own. This is the honest head-to-head: prices, the gearbox split, the service interval that quietly decides your yearly bill, the airbag gap nobody advertises, and the safety scores that **cannot** be compared the way every comparison site compares them.

## Key Takeaways

- **The Xpander is the country's best-selling vehicle** — 28,081 units in 2025, ahead of the Vios, and the best-selling MPV for three straight years.
- **The biggest spec gap isn't power, it's airbags.** The Xpander has **two**; the Veloz has **six standard on every variant**; the BR-V's top VX adds Honda Sensing.
- **The BR-V's real advantage is the calendar:** a **10,000 km / 6-month** interval means **two casa visits a year** where the other two want four.
- **Don't compare the NCAP point totals.** All three were tested — pero magkaibang protocol: the Xpander in 2018 (4 stars, 71.66), the Veloz and BR-V in 2022 (5 stars, 79.99 and 77.02). Only the star counts are a fair read.
- **The Xpander is the only one without a CVT** — a 4-speed automatic, which is both the least efficient and the least complicated choice here.
- **Entry prices are nearly identical** (₱1.090M–₱1.104M); the spread only opens at the top, from ₱1.259M to ₱1.390M.
- **All three tie extended warranty to a complete casa PMS record.** Miss the visits and you're not just risking the car — you're forfeiting coverage.

## Meet the Three

**Mitsubishi Xpander** — the market's answer, whether or not it's the spec sheet's. It looks like an SUV, sits high, and outsells everything else in the country. Mitsubishi refreshed it for **MY2026** (launched here in August 2025) with a new grille and light bar, a 3D dashboard trim, an 8-inch digital cluster, a 10-inch screen on the GLS, and a 360-degree multi-around monitor.

**Toyota Veloz** — the Avanza's better-dressed twin, on the same DNGA platform and the same 1.5-litre 2NR-VE. It's the safety-kit leader here: six airbags on every variant and Toyota Safety Sense on the V. If you want to know how it splits from its sibling, we cover that in the [Veloz and Avanza head-to-head](/blog/toyota-veloz-vs-avanza).

**Honda BR-V** — the outlier, and deliberately so. It's the most powerful of the three, it's the only one on Honda's **10,000 km** service cycle, and its top VX is the only variant here with a full active-safety suite from a brand that usually reserves it for pricier cars.

## Engines & Gearboxes: The Real Split

| | Mitsubishi Xpander | Toyota Veloz | Honda BR-V |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine | 1.5L 4A91 MIVEC | 1.5L 2NR-VE Dual VVT-i | 1.5L i-VTEC |
| Power | 105 PS / 141 Nm | 105–106 PS / 138 Nm | **121 PS / 145 Nm** |
| Transmission | 5-speed MT or **4-speed AT** | D-CVT only | CVT (6-speed MT on S MT) |
| Timing | Chain | Chain | Chain |
| Drivetrain | FWD | FWD | FWD |

Three things fall out of that table.

**The BR-V is meaningfully the strongest** — 121 PS against 105 for the other two. On a seven-seater that will spend its life fully loaded with aircon running, that margin is felt on inclines, not on paper.

**The Xpander is the only one without a CVT.** The Philippine Xpander has *never* been offered with one, including the MY2026 facelift — the CVT exists in some export markets but has not come here. So it's a 4-speed automatic against two CVTs. That costs it efficiency and smoothness. Ang kapalit: no CVT fluid ritual to get exactly right, and a gearbox any talyer understands.

**All three use timing chains.** Walang timing-belt job na darating sa kahit alin sa tatlo — that ₱15,000–30,000 surprise around 100,000 km isn't part of this segment's story.

## The Service Interval Fork: 10,000 km vs 5,000 km

This is where the BR-V makes its case, and it's a bigger deal than any styling difference.

| | Honda BR-V | Toyota Veloz | Mitsubishi Xpander |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service interval | **10,000 km / 6 months** | 5,000 km / 6 months | 5,000 km / 4 mos (mineral oil) **or** 10,000 km / 8 mos (fully synthetic) |
| Casa visits per year at 20,000 km | **2** | 4 | 4, or 2 on full synthetic |
| Oil changes to 100,000 km | **10** | 20 | 20, or 10 on full synthetic |

The Xpander's row is the one most comparisons get wrong, ours included at one point: Mitsubishi's own checksheet ties the interval to **which oil you're running** — 5,000 km on mineral, 10,000 km on fully synthetic. So the Xpander can match the BR-V's cadence, pero kailangan mong sabihin sa casa kung anong oil ang gusto mo, and pay for the better oil.

The Veloz has no such option: Toyota's schedule is 5,000 km or 6 months, whichever comes first, full stop.

## What They Actually Cost to Maintain

Straight sourcing note first, because it's the wall every guide on this site hits: **no Philippine manufacturer publishes a per-model PMS price list.** Honda's PM Cost Calculator — the one tool that gave model-specific figures — has been offline since at least August 2026. So the following are ranges from our own per-model guides, built to test a quote against. Hindi ito price list.

| | Light / standard PMS | Heavy / major PMS (40K & 80K) |
|---|---|---|
| **Toyota Veloz** | ₱3,600–5,200 | ₱7,500–13,500 |
| **Honda BR-V** | ₱4,500–7,000 | ₱9,000–15,000 |
| **Mitsubishi Xpander** | ₱5,000–7,500 | ₱10,000–16,000+ |

Read that table together with the one above it, because the per-visit price is only half the sum. The Veloz has the cheapest visit — pero doble ang dalas kaysa BR-V kung mineral oil ang Xpander at standard ang Veloz. Over a year at 20,000 km, two BR-V visits at the top of its range can land close to four Veloz visits at the bottom of its. **Ang tamang tanong ay hindi "magkano ang PMS," kundi "magkano ang PMS ko kada taon."**

The heavy stops at **40,000 km and 80,000 km** are the ones to save for on all three — that's when spark plugs, brake fluid, coolant, and filters get replaced rather than inspected, plus CVT service on the two that have one. Budget for them in advance para hindi ka ma-shock.

## Safety: The Airbag Gap and the Score Trap

Here's the specification that should move more buyers than it does.

| | Mitsubishi Xpander | Toyota Veloz | Honda BR-V |
|---|---|---|---|
| Airbags | **2 (dual front)** | **6, standard on all variants** | Front/side/curtain; **Honda Sensing on VX only** |
| Active safety | ASC, traction control, hill start (GLS/Cross) | Toyota Safety Sense on the V | Vehicle Stability Assist all variants; Sensing on VX |
| ASEAN NCAP | **4 stars**, 71.66/100 | **5 stars**, 79.99/100 | **5 stars**, 77.02/100 |
| Test year / protocol | 2018, 2017–2020 protocol | 2022, 2021–2025 protocol | 2022, 2021–2025 protocol |

**Two airbags, in 2026, on the best-selling vehicle in the country.** That's per Mitsubishi Philippines' own specification page, across the whole range. The Veloz gives you six as standard on the ₱1.104M entry variant. If safety equipment is what your money is for, that comparison is not close.

**Now the trap, and this is the part most comparisons get wrong.** It's tempting to line up 79.99 against 77.02 against 71.66 and rank them. Don't. The Xpander's score comes from a **2018 test under the 2017–2020 protocol**, which didn't include a motorcyclist-safety pillar at all — a category that matters enormously here and that the other two were scored on. Even between the Veloz and BR-V, both 5-star and both under the 2021–2025 protocol, ASEAN NCAP published their pillar scores on **different reporting bases** (the BR-V's as 26.98/32, 44.53/51, 16.5/21, 8.1/16; the Veloz's on a /40, /20, /20, /20 basis). Magkaibang exam, magkaibang scoring sheet.

**The honest read:** the star counts are comparable — two 5-stars and a 4-star. The point totals are not, and the Xpander's number in particular is answering an older, easier set of questions.

## Fuel Economy: Be Careful What You Compare

No controlled test has run all three together, so most of the numbers you'll find online are aggregator estimates. Here's what's actually citable:

- **Mitsubishi published an AAP-supervised eco-run** where the Xpander returned **21.01 km/L (GLX MT)** and **22.94 km/L (GLS AT)** over about **236.58 km** of SCTEX and TPLEX, using Department of Energy fuel-economy parameters.
- **Top Gear PH's real-world BR-V test** returned around **10 km/L in mixed driving** and **13 km/L fully loaded on the expressway.**

Those two numbers are not in conflict, and putting them side by side is the point: **an eco-run figure is a controlled highway ceiling, not a commute number.** The same car in EDSA at 5pm will not see 22 km/L. Expect all three to land in a broadly similar range in daily traffic, with the two CVT cars holding a modest theoretical edge over the Xpander's 4-speed.

## Known Issues & Open Recalls

All three are mainstream, well-sorted family cars, and none has a widespread mechanical failure pattern in Philippine sources. What they do have is **open recall history you must check on a used unit** — free to fix, minutes to verify:

- **Mitsubishi Xpander:** a **Denso fuel-pump recall** covering units purchased **May 2018 – October 2019**, where the pump component could swell and stall the engine. Free replacement.
- **Honda BR-V:** included in Honda Cars PH's **10,136-unit fuel-pump recall of 11 June 2020** (2018–2019 models), later **expanded in April 2021 by a reported 10,863 more vehicles**, with 3,094 BR-V units among them. We flag the expansion as media-reported rather than manufacturer-confirmed — which is exactly why the VIN check matters on 2020–2021 units.
- **Toyota Veloz:** three PH campaigns — a **brake cylinder slide-pin boot** issue (January 2024, 3,511 units built September–December 2022), a **front side-impact beam welding** recall shared with the Avanza, and a **front shock-absorber nut** recall shared with the Avanza and Yaris Cross.

Beyond recalls, the livability notes worth knowing before you sign: the **Xpander** feels leisurely fully loaded and its rear suspension floats a little over bad pavement; the **Veloz** shares that loaded-and-uphill hesitancy and gets noisy at highway speed; the **BR-V** has the segment's most-cited road-noise complaint and no telescopic steering adjustment. Wala sa mga iyan ang depekto — normal trade-offs lang ng murang pitong-upuan.

## Which Should You Buy?

- **If you want the safest-equipped car for the money:** the **Toyota Veloz**. Six airbags standard from the ₱1.104M entry variant, Toyota Safety Sense on the V at ₱1,262,000 — over ₱100,000 less than the BR-V's Sensing variant. This is the clearest value argument in the comparison.
- **If you want the lowest yearly running cost and the most power:** the **Honda BR-V**. Two casa visits a year instead of four, 121 PS instead of 105. Just go in knowing the third row is for kids and the cabin is the noisiest here.
- **If you flood, if you drive provincial roads, or if resale confidence matters most:** the **Mitsubishi Xpander**. It rides highest, it's the country's number-one seller with the dealer reach that implies, and it's the simplest drivetrain of the three. Tanggapin mo lang ang dalawang airbag — that's the price of entry.
- **If you want the SUV look without leaving the segment:** the **Xpander Cross** at ₱1,378,000, though at that money the BR-V VX with Honda Sensing is ₱12,000 away and brings a genuinely different safety proposition.

## What About the Avanza, Rush, and Ertiga?

The three above are the shortlist, but they're not the whole segment. The [Toyota Avanza](/blog/toyota-avanza-pms-guide) is the Veloz's mechanical twin at a lower price and finished **third in the country's 2025 sales** — if the Veloz's kit is more than you need, that's your move, and the [Avanza's used-market case](/blog/toyota-avanza-2018-review) is strong too. The [Toyota Rush](/blog/toyota-rush-pms-guide) trades MPV packaging for a more SUV-like body on the same 5,000 km cycle. And if seven seats aren't actually a requirement, the five-seat crossovers — [Mitsubishi's Xforce](/blog/mitsubishi-xforce-pms-guide) among them — give you more car for the same money.

## The Verdict

On the spec sheet these three are the same car. In ownership they're three different bets.

The **Veloz** is the one we'd point most families to, because six standard airbags and a 5-star rating under the current protocol is the kind of thing you only get to buy once, at purchase — hindi mo na iyan maidadagdag mamaya. The **BR-V** is the one that wins on arithmetic, and if you drive 20,000 km a year, halving the casa visits is a real, compounding saving. The **Xpander** is the one the market has already chosen, and the reasons are legitimate: it rides high, it's simple, it's everywhere, and it holds its value. Two airbags in 2026 is the tax you pay for that.

Ang totoong panalo dito ay ang bumibili na alam kung ano ang binabayaran niya. Read the interval, count the airbags, check the protocol year on the safety score, and run the VIN before you sign. Iyon ang buong laro.
