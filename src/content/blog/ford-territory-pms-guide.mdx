The **Ford Territory** is the Blue Oval's most approachable car in the Philippines — a compact crossover priced against the Corolla Cross and the Xforce rather than against pickups, and the model that has quietly done the most to keep Ford relevant to ordinary families here. It is also the most misunderstood car in Ford's local lineup, and the confusion is almost entirely about the gearbox.

Here is the short version, and it is the reason this guide exists: **Ford Philippines' brochure calls the Territory's transmission a "7-Speed Automatic Transmission with Rotary E-Shifter." It is not a conventional automatic. It is a wet-type dual-clutch transmission** — a DCT. That matters for what you service, what you budget, and what you ask the dealer before you sign. This is the Territory companion to our [complete guide to PMS meaning, schedule and cost](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide).

## Key Takeaways

- **The PMS interval is once a year: every 12 months or 10,000 km**, whichever comes first — the same aftersales schedule Ford PH applies across the [Ranger](/blog/ford-ranger-pms-guide) and [Everest](/blog/ford-everest-pms-guide). Against rivals on a 5,000 km cycle, that is genuinely fewer trips.
- **Units bought from January 1, 2024 onward get free labour on the 1st and 2nd PMS** (12 months/10,000 km and 24 months/20,000 km).
- **Ford is the one brand here that effectively puts a peso figure on maintenance.** Its prepaid **Scheduled Service Plan (SSP)** was bundled free with the Territory in 2024, and Ford quoted the package at "**up to PHP 43,000**" in cash terms. No Toyota, Mitsubishi, Honda, Isuzu, Suzuki or Nissan model in this market has a published equivalent.
- **The gearbox is a wet-type 7-speed DCT, not a torque-converter automatic** — Ford PH simply does not use the letters "DCT" in its marketing, and the history behind that choice is worth knowing.
- **Three different transmissions in six years.** CVT (2020–2022), 7-speed wet DCT (2023 onward), and a 2-speed DHT in the Hybrid (August 2025 onward). Which Territory you buy changes what you are maintaining.
- **Safety improved dramatically between generations:** 3 stars at C-NCAP for the first-gen car, **5 stars and 86.8/100** for the current one. There is **no ASEAN NCAP rating** for the Territory at all.
- **We are not going to tell you whether it has a timing belt or a chain.** Ford PH publishes nothing, and the Territory's 1.5 is not the European Ford 1.5 EcoBoost that every forum answer is actually about. Details below.

## Which Model Years Does This Guide Cover? (2020–2026)

The Territory has had three distinct lives in the Philippines in six years, and used-market shoppers need to know which one they are looking at, because the drivetrain is different in each.

**First generation (2020–2022).** Launched here in **August 2020**, built by the JMC–Ford joint venture in Nanchang, China, and based on the JMC Yusheng S330. Engine was the **1.5-litre JX4G15C6L** turbo petrol, rated at **143 PS and 225 Nm** in this market, driving the front wheels through a **CVT**. This is the generation covered by the 2021 recall below, and the one that scored **3 stars** at C-NCAP.

**Second generation (2023 onward).** Launched at the **Manila International Auto Show on 13 April 2023**. New engine — the **1.5-litre 4G15F6C**, now **160 PS and 248 Nm** — and, critically, the CVT was replaced by a **7-speed wet dual-clutch**. Trims launched as Titanium and Titanium X, with **Sport added in May 2024**. This generation scored **5 stars at C-NCAP with 86.8/100**.

**Hybrid (August 2025 onward).** Ford PH launched the **Territory Hybrid on 8 August 2025** — a 1.5-litre Miller-cycle turbo with a power-split hybrid system and a **2-speed DHT**, in **Trend** and **Titanium X** trims, with a **Platinum** trim added in **August 2026**. This was Ford's first electrified passenger model in the country.

One honest note on pricing: the aggregators do not agree on the current lineup. Petrol variants are listed from roughly **₱1,335,000 (Titanium) to ₱1,625,000 (Sport)**, with **Titanium X around ₱1,599,000**; the Hybrid launched at **₱1,399,000 (Trend)** and **₱1,599,000 (Titanium X)**. Because the Hybrid Titanium X and the petrol Titanium X are quoted at similar money on different sites, and because trim names shifted between launches, **confirm the exact variant, powertrain and price at the dealer** rather than trusting any single listing — including this one.

## The Service Schedule: Once a Year, Not Twice

This is the Territory's quiet advantage, and it is a real one. Ford Philippines runs its aftersales schedule on **12 months or 10,000 km, whichever comes first** — the same policy that applies to the Ranger and Everest. Most of the Territory's direct rivals are on a **5,000 km or 6-month** cycle.

Run the numbers on a typical Metro Manila commute of around 15,000 km a year and the difference is not subtle:

| Annual distance | Territory visits/yr | A 5,000 km rival's visits/yr |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 km | 1 | 2 |
| 15,000 km | 1–2 | 3 |
| 20,000 km | 2 | 4 |

<Pullquote>Half the trips to the casa is not just money. It is half the Saturdays.</Pullquote>

Standard maintenance across Ford's schedule covers oil and filter changes every 10,000 km, with the other filters, fluids and spark plugs replaced at their own longer intervals. And there is a genuine sweetener that is easy to miss: **for vehicles bought from January 1, 2024 onward, Ford PH gives free labour on the first PMS (12 months/10,000 km) and the second (24 months/20,000 km).** You still pay for parts and consumables, but two visits' worth of labour is real money.

One caveat we will not gloss over: a 10,000 km oil interval is a manufacturer figure calculated for normal service. Manila and South Luzon traffic — long idling, short trips, heat — is closer to what most manufacturers define as severe use. Plenty of turbo owners split the difference and change oil at around 7,500 km or every six months. **That is an owner's judgment call, not a Ford requirement**, and we are flagging it as the former.

## What It Actually Costs — and Why Ford Is the Exception

Every PMS guide on this site runs into the same wall: **no manufacturer in the Philippines publishes a per-model service price list.** We have confirmed that for Toyota, Mitsubishi, Honda, Isuzu, Suzuki and Nissan. Ford is the closest thing to an exception, and it is worth understanding why.

Ford PH sells a prepaid package called the **Scheduled Service Plan (SSP)** — you pay once, up front, and your future PMS visits are covered. From Ford's own description, it covers the periodic maintenance items "from basic oil changes to air and fuel filter replacement." You are eligible while your vehicle is **under 12 months old and under 10,000 km**, and you can buy **2-year, 3-year or 5-year** packages.

Four things about it are genuinely useful:

- **It locks in today's prices** against future parts and labour increases.
- **It is transferable.** If you sell the car, the remaining SSP goes with it — which makes a used Territory with plan left on it worth more than one without. Ask about this when buying secondhand.
- **It forces the schedule.** Prepaid work tends to get done.
- **It is quotable.** This is the part that matters for anyone trying to budget.

In August 2024, Ford PH bundled a **complimentary 5-year SSP** with the Territory alongside a free 5-year warranty and 5-year roadside assistance, and stated the package was worth "**up to PHP 43,000**" in cash terms.

Now, be careful with that number, because we are going to be precise where most coverage is not: **₱43,000 is the value of the whole bundle** — plan, warranty and roadside together — not the PMS alone. But it is still the most useful public anchor that exists for this car. Five years on a once-a-year schedule is five services. Even if you attribute the entire ₱43,000 to maintenance, which overstates it, you land at roughly **₱8,600 per visit as a ceiling**, and the true PMS-only figure sits below that.

That reasoning is why our estimates below are what they are:

| PMS stage | Interval | Estimated casa cost |
|---|---|---|
| Regular annual PMS | Every 10,000 km / 12 months | ₱6,000–9,000 |
| Larger service | 20,000 km / 40,000 km stops | ₱12,000–20,000 |

**These are estimates derived from Ford's own bundle figure and from historical Ford PH pricing — not a price list, and not a quote.** Ford PH moved years ago from a fixed published price sheet to an online Service Price Calculator, whose output we could not independently verify. Get a written quote from your dealer, and if you are buying new, price the SSP against paying per visit before you decide.

## The Transmission: Ford PH Won't Call It a DCT

Here is the single most important thing to understand about the current Territory.

When Ford Philippines launched the second-generation car at the 2023 Manila International Auto Show, the brochure listed a "**7-Speed Automatic Transmission with Rotary E-Shifter**." AutoIndustriya's Marcus De Guzman flagged the discrepancy in April 2023: the PH-spec Territory, like the Vietnamese and Mexican versions launched before it, **has a 7-speed dual-clutch transmission**.

Why avoid the word? Ford never explained it, but the history is not hard to read. Ford's previous dual-clutch in this market was the **Getrag-sourced PowerShift**, fitted to the EcoSport, Fiesta and Focus. It developed a reputation for shuddering and expensive, repeat-visit repairs, drew legal complaints internationally, and did enough damage that Ford replaced it with a conventional 6-speed torque-converter automatic in the 2018 EcoSport — too late to save that model's local reputation.

**So is the Territory's gearbox the same risk? No — and the distinction is real, not marketing.** PowerShift was a **dry**-type DCT. The Territory's is a **wet**-type: its clutch packs run bathed in fluid. That lubrication lets it handle more torque and, in principle, last considerably longer, which is why wet DCTs are the norm in performance cars. It is a fundamentally better-suited design for stop-go traffic than the dry unit ever was.

But it is still a dual-clutch, and that carries three practical consequences most Territory owners are never told:

- **A wet DCT has transmission fluid that is a genuine maintenance item.** Unlike a sealed-for-life claim on some automatics, the fluid in a wet dual-clutch does real work cooling and lubricating clutch packs. **Ford PH does not publish a Territory DCT fluid interval, so ask your dealer for it in writing** and get it on the record in your service booklet.
- **Low-speed behaviour is different from a torque-converter automatic.** A slight hesitation from a standstill, or a firmer engagement in crawling traffic, is characteristic of the mechanism — two clutches physically engaging rather than fluid coupling absorbing the difference. It is not automatically a fault.
- **Heat is the enemy.** Long crawls in heavy traffic are the hardest work a DCT does. This is the one drivetrain where we would actively encourage the shorter oil-and-fluid habit over the maximum interval.

And if you are shopping used: the **2020–2022 Territory has a CVT instead**, which is a different maintenance item entirely with its own fluid requirement. The **Hybrid has a 2-speed DHT**, different again. Three gearboxes, one nameplate. Confirm which one is in front of you.

## Timing Belt or Chain? The Honest Answer

Search this question and you will get a confident answer within seconds. Ignore it, because almost every one of those answers is about a different engine.

The internet's "Ford 1.5 EcoBoost" is the European and American engine found in cars like the Focus, Escape and Bronco Sport — the family where the wet-timing-belt debate lives, and the one subject to overseas recalls such as the July 2025 cracked-injector campaign in Canada covering the Escape and Bronco Sport. **The Territory does not use that engine.**

The Territory's 1.5 comes from a different lineage entirely: the **JX4G15 family, developed by JMC with AVL** and built in Nanchang, China, appearing as the **JX4G15C6L** in the first generation and the **4G15F6C** in the current one. "EcoBoost" here is a Ford brand name applied to a joint-venture engine, not a guarantee that it shares hardware with the European unit.

So: **Ford Philippines publishes no timing specification for the Territory, and we could not find one from any source that verifiably describes this engine rather than its European namesake.** We are not going to fill the gap with a guess — the same discipline we applied to oil viscosity on the [Suzuki S-Presso](/blog/suzuki-s-presso-pms-guide), [Honda BR-V](/blog/honda-br-v-pms-guide) and [Mitsubishi Xforce](/blog/mitsubishi-xforce-pms-guide) guides, where the circulating figures came from databases describing different engines.

**What to do instead:** ask your Ford dealer directly, and ask them to point to the line in the service booklet for your VIN. If it is a belt, you need its replacement interval on your calendar. If it is a chain, you need clean oil on time. Either way the answer should come from Ford, in writing, about your car.

## Known Issues and Recalls

**The 2021 EBS bracket recall (first generation).** In **February 2021**, Ford PH recalled the Territory over an **Electric Battery Sensor (EBS) bracket** and its attached wiring harness, which could break and leave the engine unable to start after being switched off. Ford covered **parts and labour free**, with roughly a **half-day** repair. Ford reached out to affected owners directly. By later accounts the campaign was effective — Territory no-start complaints largely disappeared from local discussion afterwards, and units built after the fault was identified were not affected.

**The Saudi steering-column recall does not apply here.** After reports of a next-generation Territory steering column recall in Saudi Arabia, **Ford Philippines confirmed the locally-retailed next-gen Territory is not affected.**

**Turbo care, not a defect.** The most commonly raised Territory maintenance point in local coverage is simply that it is turbocharged: the turbo is lubricated by engine oil, spins fast and hot, and punishes neglected oil changes. That is a caution about habits, not a documented fault pattern.

**Cabin filter.** A routine item worth naming because owners notice it: the cabin filter sits behind the glovebox and is an annual-ish replacement, sooner if you get odd smells or weakening aircon.

**On the current generation, honestly:** we found **no documented widespread mechanical failure pattern** for the second-generation Territory in Philippine sources, and **no PH recall covering it**. We are reporting that as "we did not find one," not as "there is none" — the car is three years old here and the Hybrid is barely one. Check your VIN against Ford PH's **Field Service Action tracker**, which is free and takes a minute.

## Casa vs. Talyer

While the car is under warranty — and if the reported **5-year/150,000 km** coverage applies to your unit, that is a long window — **keep it at the casa and keep the receipts.** This is not generic advice in the Territory's case. Two of its major components, the wet DCT and the turbo, are exactly the kind of parts where a warranty claim is worth far more than the few thousand pesos a talyer might save you, and where a dealer's service record is the evidence.

Out of warranty, routine work — oil, filters, brakes, tyres — is ordinary gas-engine servicing that a competent talyer handles fine. **The line we would draw is the transmission.** A wet dual-clutch needs the correct fluid and a shop that has actually worked on one; this is not the place to accept a generic "ATF" pour. Same for turbo work.

Two structural realities to factor in. **Ford Group Philippines runs roughly 50 dealerships nationwide** — a real factory-backed distributor with its own parts operation, but a smaller network than Toyota's or Mitsubishi's, so check what is near you before buying. And the Territory is a **CBU import from China**, which means model-specific body panels, glass and trim can take longer to source than for a locally-common rival. Consumables are not the concern; collision parts are.

## How the Territory Compares in Its Segment

Against the compact crossovers it is cross-shopped with, the Territory's case is specific:

- **Versus the [Toyota Corolla Cross](/blog/toyota-corolla-cross-pms-guide) and [Yaris Cross](/blog/toyota-yaris-cross-pms-guide):** Toyota wins on network density, resale and the reassurance of a hybrid system with a long local track record. The Territory answers with more equipment for the money, a turbo engine, and a once-a-year service schedule against Toyota's shorter interval.
- **Versus the [Mitsubishi Xforce](/blog/mitsubishi-xforce-pms-guide):** the closest philosophical rival, since the Xforce also runs a 10,000 km interval. The Xforce is naturally aspirated with a CVT and is backed by a published Mitsubishi service schedule you can read before you buy — a genuine transparency advantage. The Territory counters with substantially more power and the SSP price lock.
- **Versus the [Honda BR-V](/blog/honda-br-v-pms-guide):** the BR-V gives you seven seats and Honda's own 10,000 km cadence. The Territory is a five-seater that spends its size on space and equipment per passenger instead.
- **Versus the [Honda HR-V](/blog/honda-hr-v-pms-guide):** the closest like-for-like — same five seats, same segment, same long interval. Honda offers a full hybrid and a longer local track record; the Territory offers more power and a prepaid service plan Honda has no answer to.

Where the Territory is genuinely hard to beat is **equipment per peso**, and where it asks for faith is **long-term drivetrain history in this market** — a China-built joint-venture engine and a dual-clutch from a brand whose last dual-clutch went badly here. The wet DCT is a different and better design. It is still a shorter track record than a Toyota CVT has.

## Who This Car Is Actually For

Buy the Territory if you drive **moderate annual distances with a lot of highway in the mix**, want a lot of kit for the money, and value **one service visit a year** over the reassurance of the densest dealer network. The SSP suits you especially well if you are the kind of owner who would rather prepay and never think about it again — and if you plan to sell within five years, the transferable plan is a real resale argument.

Think harder if you spend your life in **heavy stop-go traffic and plan to keep the car past 150,000 km**. That is the duty cycle that asks the most of a dual-clutch, and the Territory's local service history is not yet long enough to answer the question the way a fifteen-year-old Toyota drivetrain can. It is not a red flag; it is an unknown, and you should price the unknown honestly.

For the **Hybrid**, add one more consideration: it launched in August 2025, so it has barely a year of local road time. The efficiency case for city driving is strong, but get the **hybrid battery's exact warranty terms in writing** before you commit.

## The Bottom Line

The Ford Territory is a lot of crossover for the money on a service schedule that genuinely asks less of your calendar than most of its rivals: **once a year, every 12 months or 10,000 km**, with free labour on the first two visits for units bought from 2024 onward. And Ford does something no other brand in this market does — it will sell you a **prepaid plan that fixes your maintenance cost up front**, with a public figure attached to it, and let you hand that plan to the next owner.

The asterisks are worth stating plainly, because nobody else does. **The gearbox is a dual-clutch that Ford's own brochure declines to name as one** — a better wet-type unit than the PowerShift that damaged the brand here, but still a DCT with fluid needs you should get in writing. **The timing arrangement is unpublished**, and the confident answers online describe a different engine that happens to share a marketing name. **There is no ASEAN NCAP rating**, only a strong C-NCAP result. And **which Territory you buy determines what you maintain** — CVT, wet DCT, or hybrid DHT, depending on the year.

None of that makes it a bad car. It makes it a car worth asking three specific questions about at the dealer: *what is the transmission fluid interval, is it a belt or a chain, and what exactly does my warranty and service plan cover?* Get those in writing and the Territory is one of the easier crossovers here to live with.
