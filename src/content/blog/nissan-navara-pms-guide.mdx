The **Nissan Navara** is the quiet one in the pickup aisle. It doesn't have the Hilux's resale reputation or the Ranger's tech-flex, and it's the oldest design on the shortlist — the current D23 generation launched here back in 2015. But it does one thing none of its rivals do: it rides on **coil springs at the back instead of leaf springs**, and once you've driven an empty pickup over a broken provincial road, you understand why some owners won't switch. It also runs a **10,000 km PMS interval**, which is double the Hilux's — a real, ongoing saving that nobody puts on a billboard.

So: magkano ba to imaintain, at okay lang ba 'to for daily use? Here's the honest breakdown — the interval chart, the cost by stage, the timing-chain answer, what the coil-spring rear actually costs you, the engine's real weak points, and a safety caveat on the base variant that most price-list pages will never show you. This sits alongside our [full explainer on what PMS means and what each stage costs](/blog/car-pms-philippines-guide).

## Key Takeaways

- **PMS schedule:** every **10,000 km or 6 months**, whichever comes first — Nissan PH's standard "twice a year" policy. That's **half the casa visits of a Hilux** over the same distance.
- A **light PMS** runs roughly **₱5,000–8,000**; heavy/major stops land around **₱9,000–15,000**. *(Estimates — Nissan PH publishes no Navara price list. Verify with your dealer, July 2026.)*
- **Timing belt or chain?** **Chain** on the 2.5L YD25DDTi — but it's a chain with a documented rattle history when oil changes get skipped.
- **One engine, two tunes:** **190 PS/450 Nm** on Pro-4X, VL, VE 4x4 and VE 4x2 AT; **163 PS/403 Nm** on the base EL. Same block, different calibration.
- **The rear coil springs are the Navara's whole personality** — better unladen ride than any leaf-sprung rival, but they sag sooner under constant heavy loads.
- **Watch the base variant.** The 2.5 EL has only **2 airbags**, and ASEAN NCAP scored the base Navara **2 stars** while the higher-spec version got 5. Same truck, different kit.
- **A plug-in hybrid Navara Pro is confirmed for around Q4 2026** — worth knowing before you sign.

## Which Model Year Does This Guide Cover? (2015–2026)

This guide covers the **third-generation D23 Navara** — the one Nissan Philippines launched in **2015** to replace the D40, and the one still on sale today in **2026**. Eleven model years, one generation. That's unusually long, and it's the single most important thing to understand about this truck: it is the **oldest design in its segment**, and it's near the end of its run.

Two significant updates happened along the way:

- **2018 refresh** — added the Nissan Intelligent Mobility safety suite, including the Around View Monitor.
- **March 21, 2021 facelift** — the big one. New front-end styling, a **strengthened rear axle**, increased payload, a new steering rack for better feel, an off-road mode for the Around View Monitor, and a new range-topping **Pro-4X** variant aimed at the Hilux Conquest and Ranger Wildtrak. Nissan PH also attached a **5-year/150,000 km warranty** to the Navara at this point — a first for the brand's local line-up.

The 2.5L engine and the transmission choices carried through all of it unchanged, so **the maintenance picture in this guide applies across the whole 2015–2026 run**. A 2016 Navara and a 2026 Navara get serviced the same way. What changed is the safety and convenience kit — and the price list, which now spans **₱1,240,000 for the 2.5 EL MT 4x2 up to ₱2,240,000** for a Pro-4X AT 4x4 in premium paint, across fourteen listed configurations.

**One thing to factor in if you're buying new right now:** Nissan previewed the **Navara Pro Plug-in Hybrid** at PIMS in June 2026, with a stated Philippine launch of around **Q4 2026** as a 2027 model year, in LE and VL variants. It's a China-developed truck (built with JV partner Dongfeng, sold there as the Frontier Pro) rather than a straight evolution of the D23. Nissan PH hasn't said whether it replaces the current Navara or sits beside it — but either way, an eleven-year-old design facing an incoming replacement is leverage at the negotiating table.

## Nissan Navara Maintenance Schedule (PMS Interval Chart)

Nissan Philippines states its policy plainly: **two PMS per year — every 6 months or 10,000 km, whichever comes first.** (The Almera's 7,000 km interval is a documented footnote exception in the Nissan line-up; it does **not** apply to the Navara.)

| Odometer | Typical service |
|---|---|
| 1,000 km | First check-up — inspection, fluid-level and bolt-torque check. Usually free labor. |
| 10,000 km | Light PMS — engine oil + oil filter, general inspection, tyre rotation |
| 20,000 km | Light PMS + fuel filter attention and air-filter inspection |
| 30,000 km | Light PMS — oil + filter, brake inspection |
| 40,000 km | **Heavy / major PMS** — oil + filter, air cleaner, fuel filter, brake fluid, coolant check, full driveline inspection |
| 50,000 km | Light PMS |
| 60,000 km | Light PMS + filter replacements |
| 70,000 km | Light PMS |
| 80,000 km | **Heavy / major PMS** — the 40,000 km list again, plus deeper driveline and suspension work |

On a 4x4, add the **transfer case and differential oils** to the heavy stops — these are genuinely extra items a 4x2 sedan owner never deals with, and they're not optional if you actually use the low range. Ask your service advisor to spell out which of these are included in the quoted package and which are billed separately; on pickups this is where quotes diverge.

**Why this interval matters in pesos.** Over 100,000 km, a Navara owner makes about **ten** scheduled oil-change visits. A Toyota Hilux owner, on a 5,000 km cycle, makes **twenty**. Even if the Hilux's per-visit cost is lower, that gap is real money and real Saturdays. The catch is the calendar leg — 6 months — so a low-mileage Navara still visits twice a year regardless. If you drive under about 20,000 km a year, the interval advantage mostly evaporates and you're paying for time, not distance.

The figures above are estimates built from the truck's 2.5L diesel oil capacity, typical casa labor rates, and the one hard number Nissan has publicly attached to this model (below). That hard number is the **Value Advantage PMS program**. Announced in January 2023, it offers discounted major PMS service specifically for **Navara and Terra units over three years old** — **from ₱6,182**, described as a saving of up to ₱3,000+ per visit versus standard rates. Two useful things fall out of that: it's a real, cheaper option once you're out of warranty, and working backwards from the discount, it implies a **standard major PMS in the ₱9,000-plus region** — which is where our heavy-PMS estimate comes from. Confirm the program is still running when you book; it's three years old as an announcement.

There's also the **Nissan Service Protection Plan**, a prepaid fixed-price maintenance package. When a brand doesn't publish per-interval pricing, a prepaid plan is the honest way to lock in a number — worth pricing out against pay-as-you-go if predictability matters more to you than flexibility.

## Light PMS vs. Heavy PMS on the Navara

**Light PMS** is the routine one — engine oil and oil filter, a visual inspection of brakes, belts, hoses and suspension, fluid top-ups, tyre rotation. On the Navara this is most of your visits, and the cost is driven mostly by that large diesel oil fill.

**Heavy PMS** (what most people mean by "major PMS") lands at the 40,000 km and 80,000 km marks and adds the consumables: air cleaner element, **fuel filter** — non-negotiable on a common-rail diesel, and more urgent here than on a gasoline car given Philippine fuel quality — brake fluid, coolant inspection, and a full driveline check. Hindi porket mura ang gastos sa light PMS, mura na rin ang heavy. Budget for the 40k and 80k stops separately; they're roughly double.

**The diesel-specific line item to keep an eye on** is fuel filtration. Water or dirt getting through to a common-rail injection system is expensive damage, and on a truck that spends time on provincial fuel it's the cheapest insurance you'll buy. If your service advisor offers to change the fuel filter early, that's not an upsell you should reflexively refuse.

## Nissan Navara: Timing Belt or Chain?

**Chain.** The YD25DDTi uses a timing chain, not a rubber belt, so there is **no scheduled timing-belt replacement** — you avoid the ₱20,000-plus job that belt-driven rivals eventually face. (Worth noting: the [Ford Ranger's wet timing belt](/blog/ford-ranger-pms-guide) is the sharpest contrast in this segment.)

But "chain" is not the same as "forget about it," and on this engine specifically that distinction earns its keep. The YD25's timing chain has a **well-documented history of rattle, stretch and guide wear** — reported most on higher-mileage D40 and early D23 units, and consistently linked in owner reports to **poor or infrequent oil changes**. The chain is lubricated by engine oil and tensioned hydraulically by oil pressure; degraded or low oil is exactly what wears it out early.

The practical takeaway is simple and it's the same advice that fixes half of this engine's reputation: **change the oil on time, use the correct diesel-spec grade, and don't stretch the interval because "chain naman."** A rattling chain on cold start is the symptom to listen for.

## The Coil-Spring Rear End — Comfort Now, a Trade-Off Later

Every mainstream pickup sold here uses leaf springs at the back. The Navara doesn't — it runs a **5-link coil-spring rear suspension**, the same basic principle as an SUV's. This is the truck's defining engineering decision and you should choose it, or reject it, deliberately.

**What you get:** a noticeably better ride when the bed is empty, which for most private owners is nearly all the time. Less of the skittering, bouncing rear end that leaf-sprung pickups do over rough or rippled surfaces. PH owner feedback consistently names ride comfort as the Navara's standout trait, and it's a real quality-of-life difference on a daily drive.

**What it costs you:** coil springs are more prone to sagging over time under sustained heavy loads. Owners who constantly haul near-maximum payload or tow report the rear settling lower than it should — and it's a known enough pattern that the aftermarket sells load-assist and heavier-rate spring solutions for it. Nissan itself addressed this in the 2021 facelift with a **strengthened rear axle and increased payload**, which suggests the criticism landed.

**How to decide:** if the Navara is a family vehicle and weekend hauler that spends weekdays empty, the coil rear is a genuine advantage over every rival. If it's a work truck carrying steel, feed sacks or construction load every single day, a leaf-sprung [Hilux](/blog/toyota-hilux-pms-guide) or Ranger will hold its ride height longer. That's not a defect — it's the trade-off the design makes on purpose.

## Known Issues on the Nissan Navara

The Navara's 2.5L YD25DDTi is a long-serving engine with a long paper trail. That's a double-edged thing: plenty of documented faults, but also plenty of mechanics who know it cold. Here's what's genuinely reported — and where the reports come from, because the sourcing matters.

- **Turbocharger failure** — the most frequently reported YD25DDTi issue, typically surfacing somewhere in the **120,000–180,000 km** range. The common mechanism is a **blocked turbo oil-feed line**, which is directly sensitive to oil quality and change intervals. Symptoms: sudden power loss, black smoke under acceleration, whining or whistling from the engine bay.
- **Timing chain rattle and wear** — as covered above. Reported mostly on D40 and early D23 units, and repeatedly tied to neglected oil maintenance.
- **EGR carbon build-up** — the YD25 accumulates deposits in the EGR system over time, giving rough idle, power loss and black smoke. Common on high-mileage examples and a known service item rather than a surprise.
- **Rear coil sag under sustained heavy loads** — the suspension trade-off described above, most reported on earlier pre-facelift units.
- **Limp mode from fuel-system faults** — reported in connection with fuel contamination and injection issues, which loops back to the fuel-filter point.

**An important qualifier on all of the above.** These are documented at the **engine-family level**, largely from UK, South African and Australian owner forums and workshops covering the D40 and early D23, on high-mileage trucks. We could **not** find a Philippine recall, technical service bulletin or local defect pattern for any of them, and we're not going to translate overseas forum volume into a claim about PH units. What we can say is that they share one root cause — **oil and fuel maintenance discipline** — which is the most actionable thing an owner can control.

**What Philippine owners actually report** is more positive than that list implies. Across roughly **50 owner reviews on Zigwheels PH, 48 rated their purchase positively**, with recurring praise for low-end torque, effortless expressway overtaking, ride comfort from the coil rear, and decent economy for the size. Independent PH road-test economy sits around **8.3 km/L in heavy city conditions**, with owners reporting substantially better on open highway runs.

**On recalls:** Nissan PH's ongoing **Takata airbag service campaign** lists the Navara — but for **model years 2007–2014**, which is the previous D40 generation, not the D23 covered by this guide. We found **no publicly reported recall specific to the D23 Navara**. The honest caveat we apply to every PH recall claim holds here: the Philippines has no public searchable recall database, so absence of evidence isn't proof. A VIN check at any Nissan dealer is free and settles it in minutes.

## Nissan Navara Warranty — 5 Years / 150,000 km

The Navara carries a **5-year or 150,000 km warranty**, whichever comes first. Structurally it's Nissan PH's standard **3-year/100,000 km** coverage plus an additional **2 years or 50,000 km** on mechanical and electrical parts — introduced alongside the 2021 facelift and, at the time, a segment-leading term.

That's a genuinely strong number in this class. For context from our [four-way pickup comparison](/blog/hilux-vs-ranger-vs-dmax-vs-navara): the Ford Ranger matches it at 5 years/150,000 km, the Hilux offers 5 years/140,000 km, and the [Isuzu D-Max trails at 3 years/100,000 km](/blog/isuzu-d-max-pms-guide). On warranty alone, the Navara is at the front of the pack.

Keep every PMS receipt and stay on schedule — warranty claims on powertrain components are exactly where a missing service history costs you.

## Casa vs. Talyer & Parts

**Stay at the casa while the truck is under warranty.** Five years is a long runway, and there's no argument for risking coverage on an engine whose known weak points are lubrication-related. Once you're past it, the calculus changes — and Nissan itself acknowledges this with the **Value Advantage PMS program** aimed squarely at out-of-warranty Navara and Terra owners, which is a rare case of a brand competing with independent shops on price rather than pretending they don't exist.

**On parts and network:** the YD25DDTi has been in production across the D22, D40 and D23 for over two decades and in dozens of markets, so the aftermarket for filters, belts, turbo components and suspension parts is deep and cheap. That longevity is a real ownership advantage of buying the oldest design in the class.

The network is the softer spot. Nissan PH's dealer footprint is **materially smaller than Toyota's**, and it was **restructured in April 2026** with new dealer partners appointed across NCR, Cavite, Visayas and Mindanao — published counts currently vary between sources during the transition. If you live outside a major urban centre, check where your nearest Nissan service point actually is before you buy, and factor the drive into that 6-month cycle.

**Parts to keep in the budget:** engine oil (7-plus litres per change adds up), oil and fuel filters, air cleaner element, brake pads, and — on 4x4 units — transfer case and differential oils at the heavy intervals.

## Safety: Read the Variant, Not the Star Rating

This deserves its own section because the marketing number and the reality diverge depending on which Navara you buy.

**Airbags are not standard across the range.** The base **2.5 EL 4x2 MT** has **two** — driver and front passenger. Step up to VE and above and you get **six**: front, front side and curtain airbags. The base truck also does without alloy wheels, fog lamps and the driver-assistance features (blind-spot warning, lane-departure warning) that the upper trims carry.

**ASEAN NCAP tested both, and the results are far apart.** The base variant scored **2 stars** (2015 model year, 49.34 points total). The higher-spec variant — listed as "Navara HV" — scored **5 stars** (2016 model year, 75.44 points). Same truck, different safety equipment, wildly different outcome. When you see "5-star Navara" quoted, that's the well-equipped version.

**And both results are old.** They were run under ASEAN NCAP's **2012–2016 rating protocol**, which has since been superseded twice. A 5-star result from that era is not equivalent to a 5-star result under the current protocol — for comparison, the [Yaris Cross's 5 stars](/blog/toyota-yaris-cross-pms-guide) come from an August 2025 test under current rules. We flag this rather than repeat the headline number, because a decade-old crash rating on an eleven-year-old design is exactly the kind of claim that deserves a footnote.

## Verdict: A Specific Truck for a Specific Owner

The Navara is not the default pickup recommendation, and it doesn't need to be. It's the **comfort choice** in a segment that mostly ignores comfort — the coil-spring rear is a real, felt advantage every day the bed is empty, which for a family truck is most days. Add a **10,000 km PMS interval** that halves your casa visits versus a Hilux, a **5-year/150,000 km warranty** at the top of the class, and a two-decade-old engine with cheap, plentiful parts, and the running-cost case is stronger than the Navara's low profile suggests.

Where it asks for honesty in return: the design is **eleven years old** with a plug-in hybrid replacement arriving around **Q4 2026**; the base EL is **under-equipped on safety** and priced accordingly; resale won't match a Hilux; and the engine's known weak points — turbo and timing chain — both punish neglected oil changes rather than bad luck.

**Buy it if** you want the best-riding pickup here, you'll keep it long enough for the maintenance savings to compound, and you'll be disciplined about oil. **Skip it if** you haul heavy every day, you need the strongest resale, or you'd rather wait a couple of months to see what the Navara Pro turns out to be.
